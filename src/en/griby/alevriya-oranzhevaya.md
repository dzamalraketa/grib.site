## Description

Fruit body — a cup 2–10 cm, at first nearly globose then opening into a wavy-edged bowl; **the inner surface is vivid orange like tangerine peel**, the outer paler and slightly downy. Flesh thin, brittle. Grows in crowded groups, often as a "chain" along a path — a trail of spore fallout.

## On the path and the roadside

One of the few cup fungi tied not to forest litter but to **open trampled soil**: footpaths, forest road verges, even gravel. Spots recur year after year — the mycelium remembers the trail.

## Cooking

Edible — and unlike many cups, genuinely pleasant: brittle, faintly "carroty" flesh. Cooked by a quick fry or added to salads for colour. Take only young springy cups — old ones tear and taste of nothing.

## Lookalikes

- **Scarlet elf cup** (*Sarcoscypha*): on wood, early spring, scarlet (not orange).
- **Otidea** and other cups: duller, other habitats.
On a path the "orange peel" is unmistakable — a unique silhouette.
