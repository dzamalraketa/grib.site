## Description

Cap 3–8 cm, grey-brown, convex → depressed with an umbo; gills decurrent, creamy. The stem has a characteristic **bottle-shaped swelling** at the base (hence clavipes — "club foot"): the base is markedly wider than the top. Smell — "lemony" or "floral" when young, sour when old.

## Toxicity — the Antabuse effect

Like the common ink cap (*Coprinopsis atramentaria*), the club-footed clitocybe contains a substance blocking **aldehyde dehydrogenase**: combined with alcohol it causes flushing, tachycardia, nausea, headache. It is the second such species in the catalogue; its "antabuse" effect was described in Europe back in the 19th century (as *Clitocybe clavipes*).

## Mechanism

The compound (a coprine analogue) accumulates **acetaldehyde**: mushroom + alcohol within 24–72 h of eating → reaction. Without alcohol the mushroom may be conditional-edible — but the "poisonous" status on our card is for this interaction, not an intrinsic toxin.

## Lookalikes

Other grey-brown clitocybes with swollen stems — but the "bottle" shape and the lemon smell are characteristic. For practice one thing matters: if you ate it — **no alcohol for 3 days**; if you drank — check what went into the basket.
