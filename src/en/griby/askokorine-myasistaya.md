## Description

Fruiting bodies 0.5–3 cm — first **gelatinous drops** (jelly-like, pink-lilac), then opening into **irregular cups or "ears"** on a short stem-like foot. Colour — from tender pink to **lilac-violet**, translucent, gelatinous. They grow in dense colonies and often **fuse into solid "pink brains"** — coating a stretch of log in a glistening lilac mass.

## "Purple jelly" on a log

This is not a true "jelly fungus" (not a tremella-type basidiomycete) but an **ascomycete**: the cups discharge ascospores. In texture, though, it is pure jelly — transparent, springy, glossy. On dark wet wood the lilac colonies are visible from far off — one of late autumn's most "postcard" finds.

## A curious fact

*Ascocoryne sarcoides* has been studied as a source of **biofuel**: its volatile metabolites ("mycodiesel"-like mixtures) drew engineers' attention — a fungus growing on wood waste produces hydrocarbon-like compounds. No practical use yet, but "fungal diesel" is a real term in the literature.

## Edibility

Inedible — minute gelatinous flesh of no culinary value; no reason to gather it.

## Ecology

A saprotroph of dead broadleaf wood — beech, birch, willow, alder: logs, stumps, wet branches in shade. Fruits **from summer into late autumn**, most vigorously in wet October–November.

## Lookalikes

- **Ascocoryne cylichnium**: larger, dark wine-brown, more regular cups.
- **Tremellas and exidias**: similar jelly but different colours (white, black, yellow) — the lilac-pink is nearly unique.
- **Young pezizas**: cups, but neither gelatinous nor lilac.
