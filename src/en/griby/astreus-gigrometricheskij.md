## Description

Fruit body — a **ball 1–3 cm on star-like rays**: in dry weather the rays curl up around the spore sac, in wet they **unfold**, exposing the spore sac with a pore at the top. The rays' outer side is dark and velvety; the inner is pale, cracking.

## A fungus hygrometer

Hygrometricus = "measuring humidity". The rays of astraeus are **hygroscopic**: dry keratinoid tissue curls in drought and straightens in rain — pure mechanics, without "life" in the usual sense: the fungus finished sporulating long ago, yet it still moves. The rays can open and close many times a season.

## Ecology — sand and pine

It favours dry sandy pinewoods, sparse mixed stands, open sandy exposures — places where moisture is rare and every drop must be caught. That is the point of the mechanism: the "star" opens precisely when raindrops help scatter the spores.

## Versus the true earthstars

- **Collared earthstar** (*Geastrum triplex*): larger, rays thicker and fleshier, **not hygroscopic** — once open it stays open forever.
- **Astraeus**: smaller, with a clearly **black inner ray layer** and the ability to "revive" repeatedly.
