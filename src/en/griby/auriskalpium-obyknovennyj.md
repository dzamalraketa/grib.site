## Description

Cap 1–2 cm, kidney- or fan-shaped, brown, velvety; beneath — fine teeth (a hydnoid hymenophore!). Stem thin, 1–4 cm, lateral. It grows **exclusively out of pine (rarely spruce) cones**, often half-buried in litter — so the fungus seems to poke straight from the ground.

## The "cone mushroom"

The only common central-belt species fruiting strictly on cones. In winter, when almost nothing else grows, "earpicks" keep poking out from under the snow — a tiny winter fungal trophy.

## Ecology

A saprotroph on the toughest substrate in the forest — cone scales and stems rich in lignin and resins. Its niche is so narrow that the species is almost an indicator of pine plantations.

## Practice

Inedible (microscopic flesh + tough), but harmless. For the winter forest collection — the most charismatic miniature: finding one on a cone is its own small foraging feat.
