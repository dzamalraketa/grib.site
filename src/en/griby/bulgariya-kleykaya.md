## Description

Cups 1–4 cm, first closed "buttons", then opening into **deep hollow bowls**. The inner (spore-bearing) surface is **glossy black**, like black rubber; the outer is **felty-brown, scurfy**. Flesh thick, **rubbery-elastic**, gelatinous. They grow in dense clusters — dozens of "buttons" on a single branch. Old cups shrivel into black lumps.

## "Rubber buttons" on oak

Bulgaria is one of the most "tactile" fungi: the thick rubbery cup bends but does not tear — like a piece of inner tube. On the bark of a fresh oak log, glossy black bowls on a felty-brown base look like nailed-on buttons. Squeeze a cup and it puffs out a cloud of black spores — and your fingers **stain brown** (hence inquinans — "staining").

## Edibility

Inedible — rubbery flesh and no culinary value; older guides marked it "suspect". Its value is identificational and photographic: a signature sight of freshly fallen oak.

## Ecology

A saprotroph of **oak and beech** wood: it arrives on recently fallen branches and cut trunks, often right on the bark. Fruits in dense colonies through summer–autumn; dries into black lumps that hang on the branch for another year.

## Lookalikes

- **Devil's urn**: deep black "vases" on a stem, in spring, on the ground.
- **Pseudoplectania nigrella**: smaller, on conifer litter, cups not "rubbery".
- **Exidia glandulosa**: jelly-drops rather than cups; it shines differently.
