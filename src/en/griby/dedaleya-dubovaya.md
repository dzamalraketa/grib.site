## Description

Conks 3–15 cm, semicircular or shelf-like, **thick and corky-woody** (1–3 cm at the base). Upper side: grey-ochre to dirty brown, faintly zoned, greening slightly with algae when old. The underside is the key feature: not round pores but **thick winding walls forming a true maze** (daedaleoid pores) — slits and channels like a miniature Cretan palace. Flesh thin, corky, ochre. The conks are perennial, adding new layers each year.

## Named for Daedalus

The species honours **Daedalus**, the mythical builder of the Cretan labyrinth: its underside is the clearest "fungal maze" in the forest. Maze-pores enlarge the spore-bearing surface without tubes — a compromise between the smooth underside of stereum and the tubes of a polypore.

## The oak specialist

*Daedalea quercina* is an almost exclusive tenant of **oak** wood: stumps, fallen trunks, large branches. It causes **brown rot**: it digests cellulose and leaves a crumbly brownish lignin mass. Conks persist and fruit **for years** — old specimens can be dated by their layers.

## Edibility

Inedible — hard corky flesh; its value is "architectural": one of the most handsome polypores to observe.

## Lookalikes

- **Lenzites betulina**: the underside is gill-like plates, not a maze; on birch, not oak.
- **Turkey tail**: thin flexible shelves, round pores.
- **Gloeophyllums**: rusty-brown maze-gill undersides, but softer and browner.
