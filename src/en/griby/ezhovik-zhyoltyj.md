## Description

Cap 3–10 (up to 15) cm, convex becoming flattened and irregular, often lobed; cream to yellowish-orange. Underneath, instead of gills, hang brittle cream spines that detach readily from the flesh. Stem short, often eccentric. Flesh firm, brittle, pale.

## The key feature — a spiny hymenophore

A teaching species for hymenophore types: neither tubes (boletes) nor blades (russulas) — spores mature on teeth. The same trick appears in lion's mane and its relatives — a separate evolutionary branch.

## Safety

**An ideal beginner's mushroom**: there are no toxic lookalikes at all. The only similar species are edible too:
- *Hydnum rufescens* — smaller, deeper orange; also eaten.
- *Hydnum umbilicatum* — with a central dimple; edible.

## Cooking

Young fruit bodies are an excellent mushroom with a nutty, fruity flavour — a market delicacy in Europe. Older specimens grow bitter; blanch and discard the water. Good fried, dried or pickled; drying concentrates the flavour.
