## Description

Cap 2–5 cm, conical → bell-shaped, rusty-brown, with characteristic **radial wrinkles** at the margin; gills brown (rusty spore print), stem thin with a **ring** — a ring on a "conocybe" is the key sign this is Pholiotina.

## Toxicity — amatoxin in an LBM

*Pholiotina rugosa* (formerly *Conocybe filaris*) is the **fourth amatoxin-bearing genus** in our catalogue after Amanita, Galerina and Lepiota. Chemically confirmed: α- and β-amanitin present in lethal doses. Same syndrome: 6–24 h latency → GI phase → liver failure.

## Why especially dangerous

It is a "little brown mushroom" — the category foragers skip by rule as "inedible small fry". Yet exactly these have poisoned people in the US and Europe, mistaken for edible "fieldcaps" and "meadow mushrooms". The rule: **LBMs are never eaten** — and if they fruit on a garden bed or mulch near produce, pull them up by the roots.

## Ecology

A saprotroph on organically rich soil, chips and mulch — an arriving "garden" species. Fruits in groups, summer–early autumn.
