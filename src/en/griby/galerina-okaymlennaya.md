## Why it matters if you pick honey fungi

*Galerina marginata* is a small brownish mushroom on decaying wood. This is not "just another poisonous species": **amatoxins have been found in it at concentrations comparable to the death cap**. It grows on the same stumps, in the same season, as the autumn honey fungus (*Armillaria mellea*), and is gathered the same way — a basketful off a stump.

## What research shows

- Analysis of 70 *Galerina* and related samples (PLOS One, 2021): amatoxins found **only** in the *G. marginata* s.l. complex; absent from other clades. So "all galerinas are deadly" is false — but safe and dangerous species cannot be told apart by eye: the genus has ~300 species and morphology is tricky.
- Toxins are heat-stable (Beaty Museum): boiling and frying do not help.
- Clinically: onset 6–10 h (nausea, vomiting, diarrhea), transaminases rise around 36 h, liver failure ~72 h after the meal.
- A documented case (Toxicon): three people poisoned, two died; the one who sought help immediately survived, the others arrived 2–3 days later. Toxin content differed even between fruit bodies from the same stump.

## How not to confuse with honey fungi

| Feature | Autumn honey fungus | Funeral bell |
|---|---|---|
| Ring on stem | membranous, clearly visible | thin, often disappears quickly |
| Cap | pale honey, with scales | smooth, honey → ochre, striate when drying |
| Spore print | white | rusty brown |
| Flesh | firm, pleasant smell | weak, mealy odor |

**Rule: any small brown mushroom on wood without a clearly visible honey-fungus ring is skipped.**
