## Description

Fruit bodies 3–10 cm, white, spongy-corky, often fusing into lumpy masses; **bright red droplets** bead along the margin and surface — not blood but a pigmented secretion. Underneath, tiny teeth (a hydnoid hymenophore). With age it browns to dark chocolate and the "bleeding" stops.

## Red droplets — chemistry, not toxin

The droplets contain **atrumentin** — a pigment with anticoagulant activity, related to the compound found in the velvet roll-rim. The fungus is harmless to touch and look at; eating it is simply pointless — the flesh is corky and bitter.

## Ecology and rarity

A mycorrhizal partner of conifers — extremely rare in Europe and protected (red-listed in several countries). Best known in North America, where Banker described it in 1913. Its viral fame comes from photographs of red droplets on a white mushroom, endlessly re-shared online.

## Practice

If you find one — photograph it and leave it: a rare species valuable to the ecosystem. It is also the site's "textbook" hydnoid fungus: under its cap are teeth, not gills or pores — as in the hedgehog mushrooms.
