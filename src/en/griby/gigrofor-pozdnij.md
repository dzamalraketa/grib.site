## Description

Cap 3–7 cm, domed then flattened; covered in thick slime in wet weather; olive-yellow to brownish-yellow, darker at the centre. Gills distant, broad, pale yellow, slightly decurrent. Stem slimy too, with a faint ring zone, orange at the base. Flesh pale yellow, soft, without a distinctive smell.

## The "herald of winter"

True to its English name, the species fruits in waves through pine forests from late autumn, when the main mushroom season is over — and keeps going after light frosts, thanks to natural "antifreeze" in the fruit bodies.

## Cooking

Edible but second-tier: the flesh is watery and mild. Peel the slimy cuticle off the cap or it will slime the frying pan. Suitable for soups and as a filler among better mushrooms; not worth drying.

## Lookalikes

Late in the season it has few competitors. Distinguished from inedible slimy Hygrophorus by its yellow-olive colour and pine habitat. Don't confuse it with young slimy Suillus — those have pores, not gills.
