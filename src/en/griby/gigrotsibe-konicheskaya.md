## Description

Cap 3–9 cm, strictly conical (hence "witch's hat"), scarlet, orange or yellow, sometimes greenish-tinged; surface slightly waxy-tacky. Gills free, pale, waxy to the touch. Stem yellow-orange. **The whole fruit body blackens** on bruising, cutting, and simply with age — the species' signature trick.

## Blackening — chemistry in action

The blackening is oxidation of phenolic compounds (as in the blackening russulas), but in Hygrocybe it is so fast that a mushroom can "char" completely overnight. A cut cap blackens at the edge within minutes — a good field test.

## Ecology — waxcap grasslands

Waxcaps (*Hygrocybe*, *Cuphophyllus*, *Hygrophoropsis*) are indicators of **ancient unimproved grassland**: they cannot tolerate fertiliser or intensive grazing. Meadows hosting the witch's hat are protected in Europe as a vanishing biotope. In the central belt it is common on woodland glades and edges.

## Practice

Inedible — waxy, tasteless flesh, and the mushroom is too delicate and beautiful to pick. Photograph it: the "witch's hat" is among autumn's most striking finds.
