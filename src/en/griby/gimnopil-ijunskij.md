## Description

One of the largest "tufted" mushrooms: cap 5–30 cm, convex → flattened, **orange to rusty-orange**, with dry felty scales. Gills adnate-toothed, yellow → **rust-brown**. Stem thick (2–4 cm), with a **flimsy ring** (from the cobweb veil), rusty toward the base. Spore print **rust-brown** — hence "rustgill".

## The "laughing gym"

In some regions (parts of Japan and North America) the mushroom contains **psychoactive compounds** (traces of psilocybin or related substances) — hence "laughing gym": poisoning is accompanied by involuntary laughter, hallucinations and disinhibition. European populations are weaker or inactive — but the **gastrointestinal toxicity is everywhere**: bitter flesh, nausea, vomiting.

## Why dangerous

- **Bitterness and GI syndrome** — vomiting, diarrhoea.
- **A psychoactive component** in some populations — unpredictable neurotropic effects.
- Looks "edible": a giant meaty mushroom tempts people to "take plenty".

## Ecology

A saprotroph and weak parasite: bases of living oaks and other broadleaf trees, stumps, logs. Fruits in **enormous dense bouquets** — a "tuft" can weigh kilograms and is visible from far off. Summer to autumn.

## Lookalikes

- **Pholiota species**: brown scales, different gill colour.
- **Sulphur tuft**: smaller, yellow-green gills, no ring.
- **Honey fungus**: dark scales, white print, no rusty tone.
