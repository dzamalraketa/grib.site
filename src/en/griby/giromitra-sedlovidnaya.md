## Description

Cap 3–9 cm, **saddle-shaped or irregularly brain-like**, two- or three-lobed, **dark brown to chestnut-chocolate**, paler and slightly felty underneath; the margin usually fused to the stem ("a cap seated on a stem" — hence infula, a "headband"). Stem 2–6 cm, **white or cream**, often flattened, smooth or faintly grooved. Flesh thin, brittle. Spore print white.

## The autumn counterpart of the false morel

*Gyromitra infula* is the autumn "answer" to the spring [false morel](/en/griby/strochok-obyknovennyj/): the same dangerous chemistry, a different season and substrate. It fruits **on wood** (rotten logs, buried wood) rather than soil — the field key. The shape is more "saddle" than "brain".

## Toxicity: gyromitrin

Contains **gyromitrin** — a hydrazinic toxin that converts in the body to monomethylhydrazine (the same class as rocket fuel): it damages the liver and nervous system and can be lethal in high doses. Boiling reduces but **does not guarantee** removal — gyromitrin partly remains in the mushroom tissue. Human susceptibility varies: one person gets a mild upset, another severe poisoning. Modern guides (and our [hydrazinic-toxins review](/en/issledovaniya/gyromitrin-morels-verpa-warning-2020/)) class it as **inedible**.

## Ecology

A wood saprotroph: it decomposes rotten logs of coniferous and broadleaf trees; sometimes on wood-rich soil. Fruits **late summer through autumn** (August–November), as the main season winds down.

## Lookalikes

- **Helvella species**: usually grey/black saddles on a grey ribbed stem; gyromitra is chestnut-brown on white.
- **Spring false morel**: spring-fruiting, brain-like, on soil.
- **Early morel (Verpa)**: spring, a thimble "slipped" over a long stem.
