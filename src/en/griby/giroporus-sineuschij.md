## Description

Cap 5–12 cm, straw-yellow or ochre, faintly velvety; tubes white becoming yellow, **bluing instantly on pressure**. Stem pale yellow, **hollow within** with age — a chamber inside the stem — the signature of the genus Gyroporus.

## Instant blue

The bluing reaction here is faster than in any other bolete: a cut surface blues in seconds. The mechanism is the same — oxidation of variegatic acid — but *G. cyanescens* holds more reagent than most "bruisers".

## Versus similar boletes

- **Slippery jacks and boletes**: not hollow inside, bluing slower or absent.
- **Duboviks**: red pores + a stem network; gyroporus pores are pale, no network.
- **Chestnut bolete** (*G. castaneus*): brown cap, weak bluing.

## Cooking

Edible and good — but rare: here it occurs locally and in small numbers. Fried and pickled like any bolete; the bluing on cutting is just chemistry, it does not affect taste.
