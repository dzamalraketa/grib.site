## Description

Cap 6–15 (up to 20) cm, convex then flat with a wavy margin and a characteristic cloudy bloom; ash-grey to brownish-grey, paler at the edge. Gills crowded, cream, slightly decurrent. The smell is **distinctly floury-perfumed** — some find it pleasant, others nauseating; the species can be recognised blindfolded by it.

## Edibility is debatable

In Russian practice — conditionally edible: parboil 15–20 minutes, discard the water, then fry or salt. Even cooked it causes nausea, sweating and GI upset in some people — an individual reaction. **Poisonous raw** (contains a heat-labile toxin).

Modern Western guides file it under "not recommended": with so many better mushrooms around, the risk of individual intolerance isn't worth the modest flavour.

## The real danger — lookalikes

- **Ivory funnel (*Clitocybe rivulosa*)** — small, white, contains **muscarine**; deadly. Our species is larger and greyer with a strong smell, but young specimens can confuse.
- **Livid pinkgill (*Entoloma sinuatum*)** — large and fleshy, similar grey-brown; causes severe gastrointestinal poisoning. Distinguished by pink spore print (gills pinken with age) and the lack of the perfumed smell.

## Practice

If you pick it — only large, mature specimens with the pronounced floury smell; parboil, discard the water. At the first sign of intolerance, drop this mushroom permanently: individual tolerance is unpredictable.
