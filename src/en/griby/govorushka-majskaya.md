## Description

Cap 4–12 cm, convex → flattened with a wavy margin, **cream, whitish-yellow or off-white**, dry, smooth. Gills **very crowded**, narrow, adnexed-toothed, cream. Stem short, thick (1–4 cm tall), cream, often swollen at the base. Flesh white, firm. Spore print white.

## The fresh-flour smell

The species' calling card is a strong **smell of fresh flour or dough** (mealy). In England it is "St George's mushroom": it fruits strictly in spring, traditionally around 23 April (St George's Day) — a rare example of a precisely "calendared" mushroom.

## The deadly double: Entoloma

This is one of the few spring edibles — and it has a **deadly lookalike in the same season**: [Entoloma sinuatum](/en/griby/entoloma-yadovitaya/) (livid entoloma) grows in the same places, in a similar creamy colour, with a similar "mealy" smell. The differences:

| Trait | St George's mushroom | Livid entoloma |
|---|---|---|
| Gills | very crowded, **cream** | sparse, **turning pink** with age |
| Spore print | white | **pink** |
| Habitat | meadows, hedgerows | more often under trees |
| Cap margin | wavy | smoother, meatier |

**Pinkening gills = entoloma = discard everything.**

## Edibility

A valued spring edible — firm flesh, excellent fried and stewed. Pick young dense specimens; old ones are "mealy" and flabby.

## Ecology

A soil saprotroph: meadows, pastures, woodland edges, grassy verges, hedgerows. Forms **fairy rings** — annual mycelial expansion gives rings of fruiting bodies, visible as a darker band of grass.
