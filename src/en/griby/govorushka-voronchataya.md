## Description

Cap 2–4 cm, convex at first, then depressed and deeply **funnel-shaped**, with a wavy margin; colour dusty greyish-white to pinkish-buff, often with a frosted paler centre and concentric zoning. Gills whitish, **decurrent** down the stem. Stem 2–4 cm, whitish, ringless. Smell faintly sweet, mealy. Spore print white.

## Why it is more dangerous than it looks

The danger is **muscarine**, an alkaloid that drives the parasympathetic nervous system. *C. rivulosa* carries several times more muscarine than *Amanita muscaria*. Symptoms — the classic muscarinic syndrome within 15–30 minutes: profuse sweating, tearing, salivation, pinpoint pupils, slow pulse, bronchospasm, vomiting and diarrhoea. Severe cases show falling blood pressure and respiratory distress. The specific antidote is **atropine** — call an ambulance and start gastric lavage.

## The fairy ring lookalike trap

Most poisonings come from exactly this confusion:

- **Fairy ring mushroom** (*Marasmius oreades*): gills are **widely spaced and do NOT run down** the stem; cap tan with a central bump; stem tough and wiry.
- **Fool's funnel**: gills are **crowded and decurrent**; cap funnel-shaped, greyish-white; stem soft.

Safety rule: from a fairy ring take only mushrooms whose gills are clearly sparse and free — and check each specimen individually, never in handfuls.

## Other lookalikes

- **Clitocybe dealbata** (ivory funnel): a near-twin, equally muscarinic; often treated as a synonym/form.
- Giant funnels and other pale clitocybes: much larger, but white print plus funnel shape is a shared "leave it" signal.

## Ecology

A saprotroph of humus-rich soils, no mycorrhiza. Fruits in arcs and rings as the mycelium spreads outward — hence the dangerous cohabitation with the edible fairy ring mushroom. Widespread across temperate Europe and North America, literally underfoot on lawns.
