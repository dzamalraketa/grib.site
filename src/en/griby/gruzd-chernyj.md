## Identification

Olive-brown to blackish cap 5–15 cm, slimy when wet; very acrid white latex that slowly turns the flesh greenish. With birch in damp boreal forests.

## Edibility — conditional, with a caveat

Salted after soaking and boiling in Russian and Finnish tradition. However, *L. necator* contains **necatorin** — strongly mutagenic in the Ames test (PMID 6348529); later work showed it degrades during drying, storage and cooking (PMID 6547686), sharply reducing but not always eliminating activity. Bacterial mutagenicity is not proven human carcinogenicity, yet several Scandinavian guides recommend avoiding the species.

## In practice

- Dark cap + acrid latex that greens + wet birch site = ugly milkcap.
- If you pickle it at all: only after thorough soaking and boiling — and do not make it a staple.
- Confusion with *L. turpis* is harmless — equally acrid and conditional; some taxonomists merge the two.
