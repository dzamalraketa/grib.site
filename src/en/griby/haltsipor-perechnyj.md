## Description

Cap 3–8 cm, rusty-brown, convex → flat, slightly slimy; tubes yellow → rusty, pores round, faintly bluing on pressure. Stem thin, with yellow mycelium at the base. Flesh yellow, faintly pinking on cut.

## The taste — pure pepper

*C. piperatus* contains **chalcipin** — a compound with a burning-pepper taste stronger than black pepper. In Germany and Eastern Europe it has long been **dried and ground as a condiment**: a pinch in soup and the nose sneezes. This is a "conditional" edibility of a special kind — not "boil-and-drain" but "a little, as a spice".

## Ecology — a possible parasite of fly agarics

An unusual detail: the peppery bolete often fruits where fly agarics are many — and there is a hypothesis (still unproven) that it **parasitises the amanita mycorrhiza**, as the slimy spike does the weeping bolete's. If so, it is one more fungus feeding on another's symbiosis — a theme we trace from the slimy spike to the copper spike.

## Practice

Edible as a **condiment**, not a mushroom: not worth eating whole — too hot. Gather a few, dry, grind — you get natural "mushroom pepper". The accumulated bitter dose is pest defence: slugs leave it alone.
