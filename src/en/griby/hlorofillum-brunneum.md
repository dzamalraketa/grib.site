## Description

Cap 8–20 cm, white with brown scales, turning almost wholly brown with age. Gills white — staying white (unlike the grey-green gills of *C. molybdites*); **spore print white**. Stem with a ring, **bulbous at the base**. Flesh **reddens rapidly and profusely** when cut — especially at the stem base.

## Versus the parasols

The main trap: *C. brunneum* prints **white**, like edible parasols — the "green print = poison" rule does not apply here. The marks:

| Feature | C. brunneum | Shaggy parasol (C. rhacodes) |
|---|---|---|
| Stem | bulbous-based, smooth | slender, no bulb |
| Flesh reddening | instant, profuse, mostly in stem | slow, mainly in cap |
| Smell | unpleasant, "gassy" | pleasant, mushroomy |

## Poisoning

Causes severe gastroenteritis (the same symptom family as *molybdites*): vomiting, diarrhea, cramps 1–3 hours in. In Europe it is a frequent cause of "parasol" poisonings — the fungus fruits in gardens and parks where people look for edible parasols.

## Practice

If a "parasol" reddens at the stem base when cut and stands on a swollen stem — discard it. Edible parasols blush slowly and chiefly in the cap; toxic brunneum — fast and below.
