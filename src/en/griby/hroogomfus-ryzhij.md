## Description

Cap 3–8 cm, convex, **copper-red** with a darker centre, slimy in rain; gills deeply decurrent, smoky-violet in age (dark violet, almost black spores). Stem conical, orange-red at the base, with a gelatinous ring-zone.

## Mycorrhizal parasite of the weeping bolete

Like the slimy spike, *Chroogomphus* makes no mycorrhiza of its own — it **parasitises the mycelium of *Suillus granulatus***, tapping its symbiosis with pine. So finding a "copper spike" = finding the weeping bolete: they fruit at the same spots, nearly always together.

## Versus the bolete and the slimy spike

- **Weeping bolete**: a tube layer (pores), not gills; chroogomphus has true decurrent gills.
- **Slimy spike** (*Gomphidius glutinosus*): grey-violet cap, white stem with a slimy ring; the copper spike is all rust-red, and its gills darken nearly to black.

## Cooking

Edible — but like the slimy spike, needs the cap slime peeled and fast processing (it darkens quickly). Fried or pickled; the flesh turns dark violet on cooking — normal, do not be alarmed.
