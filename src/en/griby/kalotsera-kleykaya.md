## Description

Fruit bodies 4–10 cm, bright yellow to orange, branching "antlers" with blunt forks; the surface sticky, gelatinous, rubbery — hence "viscosa". They grow in tufts from a single spot on buried conifer wood.

## A jelly fungus

Calocera belongs to the **jelly fungi** (Dacrymycetes) — a group whose fruit bodies are gelatinous and can dry out and revive on wetting. The stagshorn is the largest and most conspicuous of them in the central belt.

## Lookalikes

All similar yellow "antlers" (*Calocera furcata*, *C. cornea* — smaller) are likewise inedible and harmless. Do not confuse it with the edible yellow Ramaria: that one is dry, brittle and fleshy — the stagshorn is gelatinous rubber.

## Practice

Leave it to the forest: a rare, beautiful fungus, not for the kitchen. But it is instructive — the clearest way to see a jelly fungus at work.
