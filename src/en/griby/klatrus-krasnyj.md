## Description

The fruit body develops from a white "egg" 3–5 cm: the shell splits and a **scarlet latticed sphere** up to 10–15 cm rises out — a lace "cage" of fused ribs. The inner surfaces carry a dark olive slime with the smell of **rot and carrion**.

## Why the stench

As in all stinkhorns, the foul slime is advertising for flies: they home in on the smell of decay, land on the lattice, foul their feet in the spore mass and carry the spores away. The fungus does not bloom and is not fragrant — it stinks on purpose.

## Range

A warmth-loving southern species: Mediterranean, Crimea, Caucasus, the Black Sea coast, gardens and mulched parks. In recent decades it has been expanding into cooler zones. In the central belt — a rare exotic find.

## Practice

Inedible — and for photographing, not picking: the scarlet cage against green foliage looks alien. If you find one in a garden, don't be alarmed by the smell: the fungus is harmless and vanishes as fast as it appears (the fruit body lives a day or two).
