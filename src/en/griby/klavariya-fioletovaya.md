## Description

Fruit body 2–5 cm tall — a bushy "coral twig" with numerous branches from a shared base; colour **saturated violet-lilac (amethyst)**, paler at the base. The branching "fingers" are round in cross-section, tips the same colour. They grow **in tufts** (caespitose) — several "twigs" from one point. Spore print white, spores warty.

## Violet coral — not "just pretty" but a signal

*C. zollingeri* is one of the few **violet** fungi: the saturated amethyst colour shows across a meadow for tens of metres. Like the waxcaps (our *Hygrocybe*), it is an indicator of **old unfertilised grassland**: nitrogen dressing kills the clavaria along with the whole "waxcap" community. In several countries it is a listed declining species.

## Three corals of the catalogue — three colours

| Species | Colour | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Ramaria flava | lemon-yellow | conditional edible |
| Ramaria formosa | pink-yellow | **poisonous** |
| **Clavaria zollingeri** | **amethyst** | inedible, rare |

Clavaria is the smallest and the most violet of the three: "tufts" of twigs to 5 cm against the "trees" of Ramaria.

## Edibility

Inedible — thin, brittle and bitter flesh; its rarity also makes collecting unethical. Its value is as the "calling card" of an old meadow: find an amethyst coral and the grass has seen no fertiliser.

## Ecology

A saprotroph on the humus soil of old meadows, pastures and occasionally woodland glades. It fruits in tufts — "coral bouquets" on bare earth. Vulnerable: vanishes when fertiliser is applied or the meadow is ploughed — the same conflict as the waxcaps.
