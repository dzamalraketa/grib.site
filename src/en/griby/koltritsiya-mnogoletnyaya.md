## Description

Fruiting bodies are **terrestrial funnel-shaped "shelves"** 3–10 cm on a short central stem: a cap-funnel with a thin wavy edge. Upper side: **velvety-felty with concentric zones** — golden-brown, rusty, ochre, greyish bands; young ones show a golden "tiger's eye" (hence the name). Underside: a **tube layer**, brownish, with small angular pores. Flesh thin, tough, corky. Fruiting bodies often fuse into "rosettes" — a cluster of funnels like a bouquet.

## A polypore on the ground

Most polypores are wood brackets; *Coltricia perennis* is a **terrestrial polypore**: the fruit body stands on a stem on the ground like a gilled mushroom. A rare "bridge" between brackets and cap fungi: the tubed underside is there, but the form is a funnel on a stem. "Tiger's eye" — for the golden-rusty zoned bands.

## Ecology

A soil saprotroph on **poor sandy** ground: conifer woods, heaths, bog margins, bare soil by roads. The mycelium is in the soil — decomposing organic matter, not wood. Summer–autumn.

## Edibility

Inedible — tough corky flesh; the interest is identificational and "architectural": the only "polypore flower" on sand.

## Lookalikes

- **Earthfan** (*Thelephora terrestris*): leathery funnels, but the underside is **warty**, not tubed.
- **Other coltricias**: smaller or on wood; perennis is the big terrestrial "eye".
- **Young hydnellums**: purple-brown, also leathery funnels.
- **Horn of plenty** (*Craterellus*): an edible "horn", different flesh and habitat.
