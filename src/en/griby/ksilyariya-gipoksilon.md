## Description

Fruit bodies — miniature "candles" 2–7 cm tall, branching like tiny antlers. Colour **black** (a charcoal-black stroma), but **the tips are white in spring–summer** — this is the asexual (conidial) phase, looking like "ash on a candle-end". By late season the white is gone — the stroma blackens entirely. Inside is a hard white core (hence *hypoxylon* — "under the wood"). The mature surface is dotted with black perithecial bumps.

## A candle-end on every stump

*X. hypoxylon* is among the most abundant ascomycetes: black "fingers with white tips" are on almost every old stump and log in deciduous woodland. The white tip is not mould or "flowering" but the **conidial phase**: the fungus first sheds asexual spores like "ash", then blackens and shifts to sexual reproduction — "the candle burns down".

## Sibling of "dead man's fingers"

Same genus as *X. polymorpha* (dead man's fingers — in our catalogue): but *hypoxylon* is smaller, **branched**, and carries the seasonal white "ash". *Polymorpha* is a stout "club" on stumps; *hypoxylon* a slender "candle" on branches and stumps.

## Edibility

Inedible: wire-tough stromata with no flesh. Its value is ecological (a decomposer) and photogenic: black "candles" with white "candle-end" tips are a perfect macro subject.

## Ecology

A saprotroph of dead hardwood — stumps, branches, fallen trunks; less often conifers. A "finisher" fungus: it arrives on wood already worked over by other species and eats the leftovers. A cosmopolitan from temperate forest to the tropics.
