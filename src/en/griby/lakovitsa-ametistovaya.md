## Description

The whole fruit body is **amethyst-violet**: cap 2–6 cm, convex then wavy; gills distant, broad, thick, the same colour; stem fibrous, violet. In dry weather and with age the colour fades to pale lilac — a "deceiver" because it can impersonate other species.

## Versus other purples

- **Wood blewit** (*Lepista nuda*): larger, fleshy, crowded gills — laccaria's are distant and thick.
- **Violet webcap** (*Cortinarius alboviolaceus*): rusty-brown spore print, cortina on young specimens; laccaria's print is white.
- **Lilac bonnet** (*Mycena pura*): radish smell, bell-shaped cap.

## Cooking

Edible — technically: almost no flesh, neutral taste. Not worth filling a basket, but restaurant chefs use the amethyst caps for plating — vivid wild "purple" without food dye.

## Ecology

A flexible mycorrhizal partner — spruce, pine, birch, oak; one of the first purple mushrooms of autumn and among the last of November. Loves damp moss.
