## Description

Cap 3–8 cm, convex → flat, grey-brown to dark brown, surface **felted, velvety** (hence the synonym *velutina*), with veil remnants at the margin like a fringe. Gills grey → **blackish-purple**, with white edges. Stem 4–8 cm, whitish → brownish, with a ring zone that fades to a thread. Spore print **dark purplish-black**.

## "Tears" from thin air

The signature phenomenon is **guttation**: in wet weather the fungus exudes water droplets along the gill and cap edges — it "weeps". These are not tears of grief but a physiological release of excess moisture through water pores — the same mechanism as dew drops on grass tips. White droplets against the dark gills stage the scene of a "weeping widow".

## Black print — not safety, but inedibility

The dark spore print is the shared mark of "brown-spored agarics" (coprini, psathyrellas, lacrymarias). Lacrymaria's flesh is **bitter and inedible**, and the fungus tends to accumulate heavy metals on garden soils. Not deadly toxic, but of no culinary interest — status `inedible`.

## Lookalikes

- **Psathyrella** species: smaller, more fragile, no felted cap or "tears".
- **Ink caps** (*Coprinus*): dissolve into ink — Lacrymaria does **not** deliquesce.
- **Wine-cap Stropharia** (our new card): burgundy cap, larger, edible — size and colour apart.

## Ecology

A saprotroph of gardens and parks: compost, mulch, humus borders — it loves human "garden waste". One of those mushrooms that pops up on a flower bed after rain — and "weeps" right onto the soil.
