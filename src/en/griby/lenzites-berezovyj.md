## Description

Shelves 3–10 cm, semicircular or fan-shaped, tough, corky-woody (3–8 mm). Upper side: **zoned, grey-brown or ochre-grey**, slightly hairy to felty, greening with algae when old. The key feature is the **underside**: not round pores but **thick gill plates** (lamellae), ochre-brown, fanning out from the attachment point — like a gilled mushroom, only on a shelf. Flesh thin, corky, whitish-brown.

## A polypore with gills

Lenzites is a rare "intermediate" design: a bracket polypore whose spore-bearing surface is **true gills**, not tubes. Evolutionarily it shows how fungal "architecture" solves one task (maximum spore area) in different ways: polypores use tubes, lenzites uses plates, the [oak mazegill](/en/griby/dedaleya-dubovaya/) uses a labyrinth. Seeing a "gilled polypore" is a good lesson in identification.

## The birch specialist

As the name suggests — an almost exclusive tenant of **birch** wood: dead branches, logs, stumps, worked birch timber. It causes **white rot**. The shelves live and fruit year-round; old dried ones persist for years.

## Edibility

Inedible — tough woody flesh; the interest is purely identificational — a "polypore with gills" instead of ordinary pores.

## Lookalikes

- **Turkey tail and hairy bracket**: round pores, not gills.
- **Oak mazegill**: a labyrinthine, not gilled underside; on oak.
- **Daedaleopsis confragosa**: nearly gilled underneath but more "mazey", pinking on rubbing.
- **Panellus and other wood gillies**: softer, differently shaped — lenzites is hard and shelf-like.
