## Description

Cap 3–7 cm, pinkish-brown with a darker centre, covered in brown scales on a paler ground; edge with veil remnants. Gills white, free. Stem thin, with a pinkish ring, brownish flocci below. Smell sweetish, unpleasant in age.

## Toxicity — amatoxin in town

Unlike most "little parasols" (mild poisoning or mere inedibility), *L. brunneoincarnata* contains **amatoxins** — the same cyclic peptide as the death cap, at doses that have killed in Europe. The syndrome is amanitic: 6–24 h latency → severe GI phase → false recovery → acute liver failure.

## Why especially dangerous

It grows where people pick "urban mushrooms": parks, lawns, gardens — in warm autumns, en masse. Children and beginners confuse it with small field mushrooms and parasols. The rule: **small lepiotas are never eaten** — neither in the park nor the forest.

## Lookalikes

- **Small agarics** (*Agaricus*): pink gills, brown spore print, yellowing on bruising — the dapperling neither yellows nor pinks.
- **Other dapperlings**: there is no safe "little lepiota" determination for the pot — the whole clan is off the menu.
