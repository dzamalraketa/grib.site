## Identification

Cap 3–6 cm, pinkish-beige with concentric red-brown scales; ring on the stem; white gills. Its silhouette resembles a young [parasol mushroom](/en/griby/grib-zontik/) and a champignon — that is exactly why it is dangerous.

## Why it is dangerous

Like the [death cap](/en/griby/blednaya-poganka/), it contains **α-amanitin** — a toxin that suppresses RNA polymerase II in liver cells. Fatal poisonings by this species are documented; the toxin is heat-stable, cooking does not help. The picture is the same as the death cap's: latent phase 6–12 h → gastrointestinal phase → liver failure.

## In practice

- **The mycologists' rule: parasols are picked only large** — cap 10 cm+, scales on a white background, movable ring, flesh that blushes. Anything small "looking like a parasol" is passed by.
- Late (>6 h) vomiting after any forest mushrooms — call emergency services immediately.
