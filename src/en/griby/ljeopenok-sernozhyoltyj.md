## Description

Cap 2–6 cm, bell-shaped → flattened, **bright yellow to sulphur-yellow**, often with an orange-brown centre. Gills adnate: first sulphur-yellow, then **greenish-olive**, purple-brown in old specimens. Stem yellow, sometimes browner below; no ring (veil remnants are yellowish flakes at the cap edge, a cobweb "curtain" in the young). Spore print **purple-brown**.

## The main danger: the honey-fungus double

This is **the most important poisonous lookalike of edible honey fungi** (autumn and winter species). It grows in the same dense "bouquets" on stumps, in the same months, often **side by side with real honey mushrooms** — a forager can drop it into the shared basket. The differences:

| Trait | Honey fungus (autumn) | Sulphur tuft |
|---|---|---|
| Gills | cream → brown | **greenish-olive** |
| Ring | present (a skirt) | absent |
| Smell | pleasant, mushroomy | harsh, musty |
| Taste | mild | **very bitter** |
| Cap scales | present (dark) | absent (smooth) |

**The green tinge of the gills is the key stop-signal.**

## Toxicity

Poisonous: causes a severe **gastrointestinal syndrome** — vomiting, diarrhoea, cramps, and at high doses occasional neurological symptoms (convulsions, loss of consciousness). Toxins are fasciculols and steroids; dozens of poisonings are reported annually in Japan and Europe. **Bitter taste is a late signal**: if one lands in a shared pot, the bitterness ruins the whole dish.

## Ecology

A saprotroph, occasionally a weak parasite: it decomposes stumps and logs of broadleaf and coniferous trees — one of the most widespread wood fungi of the temperate zone. Fruits in huge dense tufts (hundreds of fruiting bodies) from May to November; in mild winters, year-round.

## Lookalikes

- **Honey fungi (autumn and winter)**: brown scaly cap, cream gills, a **ring** on the stem.
- **Galerina marginata**: smaller, brown, on moss at the wood — **deadly** (amatoxin).
- **Psathyrella**: more fragile, brown gills without the green.
