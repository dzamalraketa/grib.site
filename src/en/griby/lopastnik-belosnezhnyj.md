## Description

Cap 2–5 cm, saddle-shaped or 2–3-lobed, **white to cream**, with an inrolled edge; the stem tall, **hollow, deeply fluted and chambered** — "twisted". The whole structure resembles a caricature of a morel: a white crumpled bonnet on a winding stem.

## Gyromitrin — a family trait

*H. crispa* belongs to Helvellaceae, the same family as the false morels: gyromitrin has been **found chemically** in it (unlike *G. gigas*, which has none). Formally conditional-edible — eaten after boiling with the water discarded — but the family's toxicology makes the "conditional" label a formality: we flag it for exactly this.

## Ecology and resemblance

Grows on soil (pathsides, edges, moss) in broadleaf and mixed woods, August–October. Distinguished from a morel: a morel's cap is a honeycomb net on a hollow stem, while the saddle's is a **lobed saddle on a fluted, not smooth, stem**.

## Practice

Despite the "conditional" label, it is not worth collecting: gyromitrin + small size + closeness to the false morels — too poor a balance. Photograph it: the white "crumpled saddle" is among autumn's most unusual fungi.
