## Description

Cap 2–5 cm, two-lobed — a "saddle" or a crumpled "rag", blackish-grey to dark brown; the edges are raised, the underside paler. Stem 3–8 cm — the key character: **grey, deeply longitudinally ribbed with "pocket" lacunae** (hence *lacunosa*) — a lattice-like texture is visible. Flesh thin, grey, brittle. Spore print white, spores elliptical with a central oil drop.

## The black saddle — kinship and difference

A close relative of our white Helvella crispa: the same "saddle" cap and ribbed stem, but *lacunosa* is **dark** (black to grey) against the white *crispa*. Both are helvellas — a genus with a characteristically "folded" fruit body and a furrowed stem.

## Edibility — caution

Status `conditional`: eaten in some countries after cooking, but — as with other helvellas — **a trace of gyromitrin is possible** (see `gyromitrin-distribution-mycologia-2023`: none found in *H. crispa*, but data on *lacunosa* are limited). The flesh is also thin and tasteless — "culinary interest minimal, risk real". In Russia it is uncommon — no reason to pick.

## Ecology

Mycorrhizal (probably) or saprotrophic — as with all helvellas, *lacunosa*'s ecology is not fully settled. It favours **forest tracks, verges and clearing edges** — disturbed ground; sometimes on humus and even on scorched remains (it likes burnt soil). An autumn species: grey "saddles" on bare earth under trees.

## Lookalikes

- **Helvella crispa** (our white saddle): white, larger — a colour contrast.
- **Helvella atra/leucomelaena**: smaller, stem less sculpted.
- **Gyromitra**: a more "brain-like" cap without the saddle shape or pocketed stem.
