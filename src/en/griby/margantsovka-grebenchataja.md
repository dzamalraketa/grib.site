## Description

Cap 2–5 cm, bell-shaped then expanded with an umbo; ground white to cream, covered in **concentric reddish-brown scales** that merge toward the centre into a solid "brown eye on a white face". Gills free, white. Stem 3–6 cm, slender, with a **fleeting ring** that often vanishes. Smell unpleasant — **rubbery/gassy**, hence "stinking". Spore print white.

## Toxicity

Contains unknown gastro-toxins: poisoning presents as nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea and abdominal pain within hours. Not fatal in documented doses, but genuine GI poisonings are recorded. Unlike the deadly *L. brunneoincarnata* (in our catalogue), amatoxins here are **not confirmed** — the Phytochemistry 2022 study (see `amatoxins-lepiota-chemotaxonomy-2022`) found amatoxins only in one lepiota clade, which excludes *cristata*. But "not amatoxic" ≠ "edible".

## Where and with what it is confused

Grows **in masses** on lawns, in gardens, on compost — right beside people. The main traps:

- **Small Agaricus species**: they have a brown print and gills that pink with age — the dapperling's stay white forever.
- **Other white LBMs**: separating them without microscopy is guesswork — the rule "all small white lawn mushrooms are off the menu" never fails.

## Mini identification key

White mushroom + reddish-brown scales + rubber smell + lawn = *L. cristata*. The ring is weak and rubs off — **do not rely on it** as the only character.

## Ecology

A saprotroph of organic soils: garden beds, flower borders, verges, edges. One of the most "human-following" mushrooms — a neighbour of gardens, which is why accidental poisonings of children and dogs are frequent.
