## Description

Cap 5–12 cm, convex → flat, **ochre-brown to rusty-brown**, **dry, velvety-felty** (not sticky — rare among suilluses!), cracking in old specimens. Pores: first yellow-olive, then **rusty-brown**, large angular; **blue faintly when pressed**. Stem 4–9 cm, yellow-brown, sometimes with rusty dots at the top, ringless (or a weak rim). Flesh yellow, faintly blueing when cut. Spore print brown-olive.

## The "dry" suillus

Most suilluses are "slugs" with a sticky cuticle; *variegatus* is the exception: a **dry velvety cap**, like an ordinary bolete. It is the field key: a dry "velvet" suillus under pine is almost certainly variegatus.

## Edibility

Edible — middling quality: the yellow flesh blues faintly on cutting (harmless — a normal reaction). Good fried and in soups; the skin need not be peeled (it is not slimy). As with all boletes — take **sound mature** specimens only, not overripe ones.

## Ecology

Mycorrhizal with **pine** — dry pine woods, heaths, sandy soils; it favours exactly the poor dry sites where other mushrooms are scarce. One of the "poor-man's" fungi of pinewoods — where all is empty, variegatus is often the only one.

## Lookalikes

- **Granulated and larch boletes**: sticky cap, paler, no blueing.
- **Cracked bolete**: reddish pores, stronger flesh blueing.
- **Lurid bolete**: red pores, stronger blueing — **careful**, though it lacks the "suillus" look.
- **Bitter bolete**: pinkish pores, bitter flesh.
