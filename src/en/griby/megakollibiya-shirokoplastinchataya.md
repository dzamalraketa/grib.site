## Description

Cap 4–12 cm, convex → flattened with a broad umbo, **grey-brown to smoky brown**, with radial fibres and a grooved edge. The gills are the key trait: **very broad and strongly spaced** (large "paddles"), white, adnate-toothed. Stem 5–12 cm, whitish-grey, fibrous; at its base **white mycelial cords** ("whitelaced" — white laces) run into the soil toward hidden wood. Flesh white, thin; faintly radish/earthy smell.

## "White laces" in the soil

The finest part of this mushroom is not the cap but the **stem base**: bundles of white mycelial cords (rhizomorphs) trail down to a buried rotting log. Megacollybia fruits "on the ground" yet feeds on **buried wood** — the stem is simply a long conduit to the hidden substrate. Hence the frequent "lone" mushrooms mid-path — a log lies beneath it.

## Broad, distant gills

The sparse spacing of the gills is rare: most cap mushrooms pack them tightly. Megacollybia's gills are broad as wings and set far apart — the "architecture" of the spore-bearing layer is laid bare.

## Edibility

Edible when young — the flesh is thin and bland but safe; usually ignored for its flimsiness. Not a target, but not a threat either.

## Ecology

A saprotroph of buried and surface broadleaf wood — birch, beech, less often others. A "ground" mushroom with **wood** nutrition — like the wine-cap stropharia, but forest-bound. May–October.

## Lookalikes

- **Entolomas**: pink gills and spore print; megacollybia's are white.
- **Grey knight**: crowded gills, strong mealy smell, no mycelial cords.
- **Clitocybes**: decurrent, crowded gills.
