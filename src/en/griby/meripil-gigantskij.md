## Description

Fruit body — a **tiered "fan" of brackets** 30–100 cm, up to **80 kg**: dozens of overlapping brown-grey "fans" on a common stem. Pores white → blackening on damage; flesh pale, quickly woody toward the centre. At the foot of old beeches — the classic "wall" of fungi.

## Blackening at a touch

As with the artist's conk, the pores **blacken when touched** — the white layer becomes a black print. On the underside of an old bracket you can "write", but the drawing vanishes: the surface restores faster than Ganoderma's.

## Ecology — white rot of the roots

A parasite of the roots and base of old broadleaf trees — beech, oak, maple; causing white rot and weakening the tree. The bracket reappears in the same spot each year while wood remains: one "place" can fruit for decades.

## Cooking — young edges only

Technically edible, but only the **young soft edges** of the brackets are edible — the centre is already wood. Boiling is mandatory (raw it causes GI upsets). Honestly: not for the pot but for the photograph — a "wall" at an old beech impresses far more than a fried edge.
