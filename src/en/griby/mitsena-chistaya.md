## Description

Cap 2–5 cm, bell-shaped, with a thin translucent edge; colour lilac-pink to pinkish-grey (almost white forms occur). Gills distant, pale, with a characteristic droop. Stem thin and brittle. Smell — **radish** — an important field mark.

## Toxicity — muscarine

*M. pura* contains **muscarine** — the very alkaloid that makes the ivory funnel and the small white clitocybes dangerous. Muscarinic syndrome: sweating and tearing, salivation, bradycardia, constricted pupils, GI upset — 15–30 minutes after eating. Poisonings are rare only because the mushroom is small and seldom eaten — which is exactly why it stays in "poisonous".

## Ecology

A litter saprotroph: a leading decomposer of fallen needles and leaves. Fruits in troops, often by the dozen — an "army of bonnets" after rain.

## Lookalikes

Many similar small Mycena and Panellus; most are likewise inedible or suspect. The rule is simple: small "bonnets" on the forest floor are never collected for food — the risk/benefit is too poor.
