## Description

Cap 1–4 cm, bell-shaped, **brownish-pink to pale wine-brown**, with a grooved edge and a wavy rim. Gills adnate, whitish → pinkish, with a **dark wine-red margin** (edge-gills — a signature "edging"). Stem thin, 4–8 cm, pale wine, with a white felty base. The key trait — **red latex**: cutting the stem releases a dark red "bloody" juice (haematopus — "blood-footed").

## The "bleeding" mycena

Latex sap is rare among mycenas (most are "dry"). In haematopus the red latex flows from a snapped stem or a crushed cap — a "wounded mushroom bleeds". Not a defensive toxin, but a chemical response to injury (phenolic compounds oxidizing in air). Aesthetically — one of autumn's most "theatrical" small finds.

## Edibility

Inedible — small, watery, acrid-tasting; no point gathering it.

## Ecology

A saprotroph of rotting broadleaf wood — logs, stumps, fallen branches. Fruits in **dense tufts** (dozens of bells on one log) in summer–autumn.

## Lookalikes

- **Milking bonnet** (*M. galopus*): similar, but "bleeds" **white**, not red, milk.
- **Lilac bonnet** (*M. pura*): larger, no latex, more violet.
- **Psathyrella/Lacrymaria**: similar tufts but no red juice and no gill "edging".
