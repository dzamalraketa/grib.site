## Description

Cap 2–5 cm, reddish-brick, convex then depressed with a small umbo. Gills creamy; latex white, mild, scanty. Stem thin, cylindrical. Flesh brittle, pale.

## The smell is the whole species

The fresh mushroom barely smells; on drying it releases an **intense aroma of burnt sugar, camphor and maple syrup** — the "candy cap". The same compound (sotolon) gives fenugreek and curry their scent: in Chinese and Japanese kitchens dried *L. camphoratus* is ground as a seasoning for meats and desserts.

## Cooking

Edible, but gathered for aroma rather than bulk — the mushroom is small. Fresh, an ordinary fried mushroom; dried, a **seasoning**: a pinch of powder flavours baking, sauces, even ice cream. In the US "candy caps" sell as a gourmet ingredient.

## Lookalikes

Many similar small milkcaps (*L. quietus*, *L. subdulcis*) — also edible or harmless. The camphor milkcap is best told by the smell of a dried specimen: the "caramel" aroma is unmistakable.
