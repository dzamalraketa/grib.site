## Description

Cap 2–6 cm, convex → depressed, **pale pinkish-brown, flesh-pink**, sometimes with a darker centre and faint zoning, dry, smooth. Gills adnate → slightly decurrent, cream-pink. Stem thin (3–7 cm), the same pinkish tone. Flesh thin. On breaking — **white latex** (milk), mild or faintly acrid.

## The smell of coconut

The chief find is the **smell of the flesh: a distinct coconut** (the same chemical note as desiccated coconut and "coconut" cream). Crush a gill — and instead of a "mushroomy" smell comes a dessert one. Glyciosmus means "sweet-smelling". It is one of the few mushrooms whose aroma positively attracts humans — though the scent's natural function (repellent or vector lure) is not fully known.

## Edibility

Inedible — thin flesh and **acrid latex**: eaten, it burns and upsets the stomach. The coconut smell is a deceptive ornament: the mushroom "smells of dessert" but must not be eaten.

## Ecology

Mycorrhizal with **birch**: damp birch groves, heathy and willow moors, peaty bog margins. Singly or in small groups; the most "aristocratic" member of the birch milkcap team (alongside pubescens, torminosus) by scent.

## Lookalikes

- **Bearded milkcap** (*L. pubescens*): a fringed-felty cap edge, acrid taste, no coconut.
- **Grey milkcap** (*L. mammosus*): pine-dwelling, bitter, different smell.
- **Pink russulas**: no latex; in lactarius it is white and "weeps" when broken.
- **Clitocybe fragrans**: smells of aniseed, not coconut; no latex.
