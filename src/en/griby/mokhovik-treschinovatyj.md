## Identification

Dry velvety olive-brown cap that cracks with age revealing **red flesh beneath the cuticle**; yellow→olive pores that bruise blue; yellow stipe with red streaks, no ring. Under oak, beech, spruce — June to October, common.

## Edibility

Edible but second-rate: soft spongy flesh — young firm caps for frying and drying. The important lesson is lookalikes:

- *Neoboletus luridiformis* — **red pores**, stronger bluing; edible only well-cooked and still risky.
- Satan's bolete — pale cap, red pores, red reticulum on the stipe; poisonous.

Rule: safe “cracker” = yellow pores + red streaks on stipe + red flesh in cap cracks.

## In practice

- Cracking and blue bruising are normal for the species, not spoilage; old fruit bodies are bland — take young ones.
- A good beginner's bolete to train the eye before meeting its dangerous red-pored lookalikes.
