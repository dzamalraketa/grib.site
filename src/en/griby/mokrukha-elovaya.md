## Description

Cap 4–10 cm, bell-shaped then flattened with a low umbo; extremely slimy when wet; greyish-violet to nearly black in rain. Gills thick, distant, deeply decurrent, grey, almost black in old specimens from the spores. Stem slimy with a glutinous ring and **characteristic black blotches at the base**. Flesh greyish, pinkish in the stem base.

## Gills, but a bolete relative

Though it looks like an ordinary gilled mushroom, Gomphidius sits taxonomically next to the boletes — visible in the thick, forked gills and long spindle-shaped spores.

## Ecology: a mycorrhiza "parasite"

The slimy spike almost always occurs beside the bovine bolete (*Suillus bovinus*). Studies show Gomphidius does not form a conventional mycorrhiza — it inserts itself into roots already colonised by Suillus and siphons off part of the carbon. Effectively a predator among mycorrhizal fungi.

## Cooking

Edible but little valued: soft, bland flesh. Peel the slimy cuticle. In Siberia and Belarus it was salted and fried alongside other mushrooms. It absorbs water heavily — poor for frying, acceptable in brine.
