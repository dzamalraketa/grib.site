## Description

Cap 4–10 cm, egg-shaped → flattened with a low umbo, **orange-brown, dark tawny or chestnut**, with a radially **grooved margin** (the "grisette" trait), smooth, without white warts — the volval remnants wash off. Gills **free, white**. Stem 8–15 cm, white with orange flecks, **no ring**; at the base a **sack-like white volva** (often buried). Spore print white.

## Grisettes without a ring

Fulva belongs to the **ringless** amanitas (section Vaginatae — the "grisettes"): no ring, no warts — just a grooved cap edge and a saccate volva. This is the "edible on paper" group — yet it is exactly here that the deadly [death cap](/en/griby/blednaya-poganka/) and its kin hide the most dangerous confusion.

## Edibility: nominal

Guides list it as "edible when cooked", but the practical advice is unanimous: **do not pick amanitas at all**. Mistaking a young phalloides or virosa for a fulva is too costly — the volva under the ground is easily left behind when cutting, and it is the volva that carries the diagnostic signs. The `conditional` status is purely nominal.

## Ecology

Mycorrhizal with a broad host range: birch, oak, pine, spruce; favours **acidic soils** — heaths, peaty woods, coastal pinewoods. One of the most common amanitas where it fits.

## Lookalikes

- **Fly agaric and panther cap**: they have a ring and white cap warts — fulva has neither.
- **Death cap and destroying angel**: white or greenish caps; fulva is orange-brown. But young phalloides fruit bodies can be brownish — **the confusion risk is lethal**.
- **Volvariella bombycina**: pink gills when mature, different ecology.
