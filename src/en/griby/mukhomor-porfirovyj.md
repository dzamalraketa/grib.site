## Identification

Grey-brown to purplish-brown cap 4–10 cm with grey-brown velar warts; white gills; fragile greyish **ring**; bulbous base with a rolled volval rim, no clean sac. Mycorrhizal with spruce and pine on acid soils; July–October, abundant.

## Toxicity

The ibotenic acid–muscimol complex: intoxication, hallucinations, delirium, incoordination, 0.5–3 h onset. No amatoxins — but heavy delirium is dangerous, and children can convulse.

## In practice

- Name trap: "grey amanita" in field guides can mean this poisonous porphyry species **or** the conditionally edible grisette *A. vaginata* — the grisette has **no ring** and a striated cap margin.
- One of the commonest "grey" amanitas in spruce woods — lands in baskets aimed at "edible brown amanitas" regularly.
- Treat poisoning as for panther cap: emergency care, lavage, monitoring.
