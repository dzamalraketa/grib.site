## Description

Cap 5–10 cm, pure white, conical → convex; gills white, free; stem white, with a ring and a sack-like volva at the base. **The entire mushroom is white** — which is what makes it an "angel" and the core lesson: not every white amanita is a death cap, but every white amanita is deadly.

## Toxicity — the same amatoxin

*A. virosa* contains **α- and β-amanitin** — the same syndrome as the death cap: 6–24 h latency → brutal GI phase → false recovery → acute liver failure. Fatalities are fewer than from phalloides only because the species is rarer and less eaten.

## The main danger — confusion

A white gilled mushroom with a ring and a volva gets confused with:
- **Field mushrooms** (pink gills, no volva)
- **White parasols** (ring slides down, no sack-like volva)
- **Russulas** (neither ring nor volva)
Only an amanita carries the **full set**: volva + ring + white spore print. Find all three — leave it alone.

## Ecology

A mycorrhizal partner of conifers and mixed stands — spruce and pine especially; warmer-loving than the death cap, peaking July–September. In the central belt rarer than phalloides, but regular.
