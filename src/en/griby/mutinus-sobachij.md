## Description

The fruit body develops from a white "egg" (as in all phalloids): out rises an **elongated "post"** 6–12 cm, orange-red, thin and spongy, tipped with dark-olive slime. The smell is weaker than the stinkhorn's but still "toilet-epoxy": the same fly pollinators.

## Versus the common stinkhorn

- **Common stinkhorn** (*Phallus impudicus*): white, fleshier, with a honeycombed cap and (in some forms) a lacy "skirt"; mutinus is **thinner, orange-red**, without a separate cap — the slime sits directly on the tip.
- **Mutinus ravenelii**: nearly identical; told apart microscopically and by habitat.

## Ecology and the stench

Like all phalloids, mutinus uses **foul slime carrying spores** as bait: flies home to the "smell of carrion" and ferry the spores away on their feet. The fruit body lives 1–2 days, then vanishes.

## Practice

Inedible — and a case where the mushroom is more beautiful than edible. In cottage gardens and beds, mutinus is a sign of generous mulching; don't be alarmed: harmless, it will disappear by itself — the flies are already at work.
