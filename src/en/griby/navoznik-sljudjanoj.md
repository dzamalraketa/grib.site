## Description

Cap 2–5 cm, egg-shaped → bell-shaped, ochre-brown with **glinting mica grains** (veil remnants), washed away by rain; the edge striate, "rainbowed". Gills white → **black** (the inky gills!). Stem thin, white. Grows in tight families on wood.

## The inkcap without Antabuse

The key difference from the common ink cap (*Coprinopsis atramentaria*): the mica cap does **not** contain coprine — you can have it with wine, no consequences. But youth is mandatory: as soon as the gills start blackening, the mushroom "goes into ink" and there is nothing to eat.

## Autolysis — self-digestion

The gills of the inkcaps **digest themselves**: enzymatic autolysis turns the cap edge into black liquid literally dripping to the ground — a way to disperse spores, carried off by the "ink". In the mica cap the process is slower than in its kin, but it happens too.

## Cooking

Collect **only young** egg-shaped caps: braise, fry, drop into omelettes and soups. Deadline — the day of picking: at home the caps start blackening right in the basket. "Family" gathering is handy: one stump can hold 50–100.
