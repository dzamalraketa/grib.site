## Description

Cap 4–12 cm, brown to dark brown with fine scales, margin inrolled. Gills white then cream-brown, slightly decurrent. Stem with a swollen, **bulbous base** and a membranous ring. Fruits in dense tufts on wood and at the base of living trees.

## Versus the autumn honey fungus

Both are wood-growing honey fungi, but *A. ostoyae* is:
- darker (brown rather than honey-coloured);
- almost exclusively on **conifer** wood and living conifers;
- marked by the bulbous stem base;
- a much more aggressive parasite — the chief agent of root rot in northern forests.

## Oregon's "humongous fungus"

The species' most famous fact: in Oregon's Malheur National Forest, scientists described a single *A. ostoyae* genet covering ~965 hectares, weighing hundreds of tonnes and aged in the thousands of years — the organism most often cited as the largest on Earth. The whole giant is one mycelium whose fruit bodies surface each autumn.

## Cooking

Conditionally edible: like all honey fungi it demands thorough boiling (raw flesh is toxic) and the water discarded. Flavour average; old specimens turn tough and slightly bitter. Few toxic lookalikes — the main thing is not to confuse it with sulphur tuft, which has greenish-yellow gills and no ring.
