## Description

Cup 2–6 cm tall, not round but **asymmetrically folded** — like a rabbit's ear: one side higher than the other, with a characteristic **side slit** and an inrolled margin. The inner (fertile) surface is yellow-orange, smooth; the outer is paler and faintly downy. Almost no stem — the "ear" sits directly on the ground or on a short base. It grows in groups, often whole "rabbit warrens" on bare soil.

## An ear folded like a hare's

Otidea's shape is unlike any other fungus: the cup is not open but **folded along a slit** — as if the cup had been pinched from the side. This "hare-ear" asymmetry is the hallmark of the genus *Otidea* ("ear" genus). No other mushroom makes such a "lop-sided pouch" on bare earth.

## Edibility

Inedible — not toxic, but the flesh is thin, brittle and tasteless; its interest is purely aesthetic: russet-yellow "ears" on dark soil are one of autumn's most recognisable sights.

## Ecology

Presumably **mycorrhizal** (like most ascomycete "cups") with conifers and hardwoods; it grows on rich soil — woodland road verges, clearing edges, humus glades. It favours **bare earth** — popping up precisely on disturbed or compacted soil.

## Lookalikes

- **Otidea alutacea** and other otideas: more yellow-brown, lacking the vivid asymmetry — microscopy decides.
- **Peziza** species: regular round cups without the "hare" slit.
- **Aleuria** (orange peel): bright orange, round.
