## Description

Caps 1–3 cm, fan- or kidney-shaped, stemless or with a tiny lateral "tail"; colour cream to ochre-brown, surface faintly velvety. Gills cream, crowded, radiating from the lateral attachment. They grow in clustered "bouquets" on wood. Flesh thin, **bitter** — hence *stipticus* ("astringent") and "bitter". Spore print white.

## Light in the dark — but not everywhere

*P. stipticus* is one of ~80 known **bioluminescent** fungi: North American strains emit a faint greenish glow ("foxfire") from the gills and mycelium — faint enough to be seen only in full darkness after the eyes adapt. **European populations usually do not glow** — same species name, different genetic lines with different chemistry. The glow is luciferin + luciferase — the same enzyme principle as in fireflies.

## The miniature "oysterling"

A relative of our *Panellus serotinus* (late oyster): both grow shelf-like on wood without a stem, but serotinus is bigger, greenish and conditionally edible, while *stipticus* is a miniature bitter "dwarf". The shared "fan" habit — a fan on a lateral stem — unites them.

## Edibility

Inedible — bitter enough to spoil a dish; not toxic, just bitter. Its value is the foxfire glow in America and its elegant "fan" form.

## Ecology

A saprotroph of dead hardwood — oak, beech and birch stumps and logs. A cosmopolitan of temperate forests; clustered "fanlets" on old stumps in autumn are a typical sight. In North America — one of the main "glowing mushrooms".
