## Description

Cap 3–10 cm, fan-shaped, **olive-yellow to greenish-brown**, slimy in rain, wavy-edged. Gills yellow, decurrent down a short lateral stem or none — it "sits" on the wood. Flesh whitish, faintly bitter in age.

## The "after-season" mushroom

Serotinus = "late". While the rest of the mushrooms have gone (October–November), panellus comes out: its fruit bodies overwinter on trunks and keep sporulating right into the January thaws. In a year when "it's all over" — this is the last wild mushroom for soup.

## Cooking

Edible — but only **after boiling** (raw it has caused GI upsets): boil 10–15 min, discard the water, then fry or braise. The taste is modest; in Japan ("sarukomatake") it is a traditional winter mushroom, salted and pickled.

## Lookalikes

- **Oyster mushrooms**: white/grey, no ring in either — but the late oyster is olive-yellow.
- **Sulphur tufts**: yellow gills when young, but they fruit from summer, no ring, different build — in November you cannot confuse them.
