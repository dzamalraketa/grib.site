## Description

Cap 5–12 cm, yellow-brown, convex → flat, **greasy** (hence "zhirnyj"); the edge long holds white veil remnants. Gills dirty-white, then rusty — but this species' spore print is **paler** than a webcap's usual. Stem with a good **ring** — white, fibrous.

## The only "brave" webcap

The genus Cortinarius holds hundreds of species, including the deadly orellanine ones. Why is *caperatus* alone allowed? Two ironclad signs: a **pale spore print** (light, not rusty) and a **greasy cap**. Every other "edible" webcap (our violet one included) stands under reservation. The gypsy is the only one where the reservations lift.

## Versus the dangerous webcaps

- **Deadly webcap** (*C. rubellus*): rusty-brown cap, no greasiness, no ring.
- **Orange-red webcap**: vivid orange, ringless.
- **Other grey-brown webcaps**: without the pale print and ring — all forbidden.

## Cooking — and a mercury caveat

Edible without parboiling: fried, braised, dried — dense, "nutty" flesh. In Finland and Eastern Europe a respected autumn mushroom.

**But:** *C. caperatus* is one of the best **mercury accumulators** among fungi (BCF up to 120 — see card `caperatus-mercury-falandysz-2014`). Occasional eating is safe — but don't make it the season's staple: gather moderately and rotate species.
