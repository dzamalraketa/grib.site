## Description

Fruit body — a **bag-like "ball"** 5–15 cm, sitting on a short false root; outside brown, bumpy, "bruised". Inside, when young — pea-sized "peridioles" floating in a gelatinous mass; when old — a black dusting spore mass like tar (arhizus — "without roots").

## Dye in a bag

The name "dye-maker" is no metaphor: the black mass of pisolithus yields a **fast pigment** for wool and cloth. In folk tradition it was used to dye dark tones — the spore "tar" colours permanently.

## Ecology — pioneer of "dead" ground

Pisolithus is a **mycorrhizal fungus of the poorest soils**: sandy roadsides, spoil heaps, disturbed ground where other trees cannot grow. It helps pine and oak colonise where normal soil is absent — hence it is deliberately added to mycorrhizal inoculants for land reclamation.

## Lookalikes

- **Earthballs** (*Scleroderma*): rounder, without the pea "peridioles" when young.
- **Puffballs**: white interior, not black.
The pea filling of pisolithus is unique: nothing else looks like this inside.
