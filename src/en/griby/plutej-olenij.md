## Description

Cap 4–10 cm, grey-brown, sometimes with radial fibres ("deer" tones), viscid when wet. Gills white, **free** (not attached to the stem), pinkening with age — spore print **pink**. Stem with neither ring nor volva. Flesh white, scentless or faintly radish-like.

## A lesson in "safe gilled" mushrooms

Three marks together exclude the dangerous lookalikes:
- **free gills** — no attachment to the stem;
- **pink spore print** — gills pinken with age;
- **neither ring nor volva** — rules out every amanita.

A "teaching" mushroom: it shows that not every pale gilled fungus is dangerous — but identification must rest on the sum of marks, not on any one.

## Cooking

Edible but mediocre: soft flesh, no strong flavour. In some countries it is gathered as a "standby" spring mushroom when nothing else is out. Frying, soups.

## Lookalikes

The main thing is not to confuse it with young amanitas or volvariella: amanitas carry a volva/ring and a white print; volvariella has a volva and a pink print. Pluteus: **pink print + no ring, no volva** — a reliable formula.
