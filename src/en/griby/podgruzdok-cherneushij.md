## Description

Cap 8–20 cm, white then ochre and finally dirty brown — the mushroom literally blackens with age. Gills distant, thick, broad, with veins. Flesh firm; when cut it **first reddens, then blackens** — the diagnostic mark. Taste mild to acrid.

## The "blackening" triad

Our catalogue already holds the pair:
- *Russula delica* (milk-white brittlegill) — does not blacken;
- *R. adusta* (charred russula) — blackens at once, skipping the red phase;
- *R. nigricans* — **first reddens, then blackens**.

The three-stage flesh reaction (white → red → black) is the calling card of *nigricans*.

## Cooking

In Russian practice — a third-grade salting mushroom: after soaking and parboiling it goes into brine alongside milkcaps and valui. Not eaten fresh: dense, bland flesh that turns acrid in old specimens.

## Lookalikes

Resembles the milk-white brittlegill and Lactarius in size and shape — but those lack the red-black flesh reaction, and milkcaps exude latex. The blackening reaction rules out confusion with death caps: just break the mushroom and wait two minutes.
