## Identification

Dense cap 5–15 cm, white when young, smoky-grey then near-black in autumn; flesh pinks, then greys and blackens on cutting. **Mild** taste — the distinction from the acrid *R. nigricans*. Under pine on poor dry soils; July–October, common.

## Edibility

Edible but low-grade: bland, spongy-dense flesh that goes flabby; old books rated it fourth category — fried or dried into seasoning powder. Taste-test the cut: if it burns the tongue, it is a different blackening russula.

## In practice

- White young + grey middle + black old, all in one flush, under pine.
- Name trap: "black milkcap" in folk usage means both this russula and *Lactarius necator* — check for latex: necator bleeds, adusta does not.
