## Description

Fruit body 2–7 cm, lateral or fan-shaped; **translucent, gelatinous**, whitish-grey, with fine scales on top. Beneath — dense small **teeth** (a hydnoid hymenophore), likewise gelatinous. Grows on rotting conifer trunks and deadfall — looking like a drop of jelly with teeth frozen onto the wood.

## One of a kind

An almost unique combination: a **jelly fungus with a toothed hymenophore**. Jelly fungi are usually smooth or wrinkled, and toothed fungi are fleshy; pseudohydnum joins the two lines. A deceiver like the stagshorn: outside an "edible tooth", inside — jelly.

## Edibility — formal

The literature lists it as "edible" — true only formally: you can eat it, but why bother with a rubbery translucent mass without taste. It is not poisonous, but it has zero culinary value.

## Practice

Photograph and leave: rare, delicate and amazing. A good "teaching" fungus of the hydnoid form: the teeth underneath are the same trick as the hedgehog mushrooms — in gelatinous guise.
