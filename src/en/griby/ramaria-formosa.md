## Description

Fruit body 8–15 cm tall and as wide — a bushy "coral tree". Base and main stem **white or pale yellow**, branches salmon-pink to peach, **branch tips bright yellow** ("yellow fingers"). Flesh white, brittle, smell faintly of radish. Spore print ochre-yellow, spores warty.

## Toxicity — a real poison, not "staleness"

*R. formosa* contains terpenoid gastro-toxins causing **severe gastroenteritis**: abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting and diarrhoea within hours of the meal. Fatalities are not on record, but the poisoning is a specific species toxicity, not mere indigestion. Important: **cooking does not destroy the toxin**.

## Versus the edible Ramaria flava

The catalogue's key teaching pair:

- **R. flava** (our edible coral): the whole fungus evenly **lemon-yellow**, no pink; branch tips the same yellow, stem short.
- **R. formosa**: stem **white**, branches **pink-orange**, tips **bright yellow** — a three-colour contrast in one fruit body.

Rule: "pink branches + white stem + yellow fingers" — leave it. As with all corals, the genus *Ramaria* is taxonomically tangled — even specialists cannot always name a specimen to species, and many lookalikes have poorly studied toxicity.

## Ecology and conservation

A mycorrhizal partner of beech, sometimes oak — a mushroom of old broadleaf stands. Listed in several European regional Red Data Books: the vivid coral is casually "picked for beauty", hurting local populations. In Russia it occurs in the broadleaf zone.
