## Description

Fruit body 10–20 cm, coral-like, densely branched; limbs bright yellow, whitish at the base. Flesh white, brittle. Spore print ochre. Grows on the ground in broadleaf woods — often in whole "gardens" of dozens of fruit bodies.

## Conditional edibility with a caveat

*R. flava* is listed edible in some countries (it is even sold in Japan), but:
- **the genus Ramaria is hard to key to species** — dozens of lookalike corals;
- several species (*R. formosa*, *R. flavescens*) are documented laxatives;
- even *flava* itself upsets sensitive stomachs.

## Practice

Western guides agree: coral fungi are not a beginner's table mushroom — the risk of mistaking another coral for the edible yellow one is too high. When in doubt, leave the coral to the forest: it is among the most beautiful woodland fungi and a marker of old-growth ecosystems.
