## Description

Cap 5–15 cm, **pink-peach**, convex; the surface carries deep **pale vein-like wrinkles** (a venose pattern), hence palmatus — "into fingers", "webbed". Gills pink, adnate. Stem lateral, short, pinkish. Flesh pink, smelling of peach/apricot.

## The most "veined" mushroom

The venose pattern on the cap is a unique marker: not a net and not scales, but thickened wrinkles of the flesh showing through the skin — like veins on a hand. Beyond beauty it is functional: the veins enlarge the surface and probably help hold moisture in shady floodplains.

## Ecology — the rare floodplain dweller

Grows on dead broadleaf trunks **in floodplains** — often above water or at flood level: elm, maple, ash. The species is demanding of shade and moisture, hence rare: in Europe and Russia it is listed in Red Data Books and protection lists. In the central belt — the find of the year.

## Practice

Inedible — bitter flesh. And "protected" means: **do not touch at all**. Finding a "wrinkled peach" is a rare stroke of luck; a photo and the spot's coordinates are the most you should "collect".
