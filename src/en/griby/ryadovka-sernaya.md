## Description

Cap 4–8 cm, convex → flat with a wavy edge, **yellow-grey or grey-yellow**, often with a darker centre, dry, fibrous. Gills adnate-toothed, **yellow**, spaced. Stem 4–10 cm, yellow, fibrous, sometimes curved. Flesh yellow. Spore print white. The whole mushroom is yellow; "sulphur" here refers to the **smell**, not the colour.

## The smell that decides everything

The key trait is the **unbearable smell of gas, sulphur or rotting garlic**, detectable from a distance: chemically these are sulphide compounds close to the odorant added to natural gas. The mushroom "announces" itself from metres away — bend down and you know. The smell persists when dried, even in collections for years.

## Toxicity and the yellow-knight double

Poisonous — it causes a **gastrointestinal syndrome** (nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea). The key risk is **confusion with the edible [yellow knight](/en/griby/zelyonushka/)**: that one is also yellow-green but smells of flour/cucumber, not gas. Rule: **any yellow mushroom smelling of gas — out.**

## Ecology

Mycorrhizal with broadleaf and conifer trees on **rich, alkaline** soil — beech, oak, spruce, pine; parks and avenues too. In groups, sometimes rings. Summer to late autumn.

## Lookalikes

- **Yellow knight** (*Tricholoma equestre*): smells mealy/cucumber-like, not sulphurous; bright yellow gills.
- **Tricholoma aestuans**: similar yellow-green, but a mealy smell, not gas.
- **Other yellow tricholomas**: without the "petroleum" stench.
- **False chanterelle**: different gill form (decurrent, forked), mushroomy smell.
