## Description

Cap 5–20 cm, convex → flat → depressed; light to dark brown, covered in **large raised brown scales like roof tiles**, blackening with age. The underside bears **tooth-like spines** (not gills): grey → grey-brown, running down the stem. Stem 3–8 cm, thick, club-shaped, brown. Flesh whitish → brownish, **bitter**, with a spicy smell. Spore print brown.

## Bitter, but beautiful

Inedible — the flesh is strongly **bitter** even after cooking (not a toxin, but it makes the fungus useless for the pot). However Sarcodon is a prized **dye mushroom**: the flesh yields blue-green and turquoise tones on wool — a rare colour that dyers seek out deliberately.

## Sibling of the bleeding tooth

From the same "tooth fungus" group as our *Hydnellum peckii* (bleeding tooth): both are mycorrhizal "hedgehogs" with a toothed fertile surface and a taste for fierce bitterness. But Sarcodon is brown and shingled, not a white-and-red "bleeding" mass.

## Ecology and conservation

A mycorrhizal partner of old spruce and pine on acid soils. In Russia it appears in several regional Red Data Books; the species suffers when old spruce stands are cut. The big "brown caps" are oddly **hard to spot** against the litter — the fungus fruits singly, blending into the forest floor.

## Lookalikes

- **Hydnellum** spp.: tougher, corky, often fused; Sarcodon is softer and scalier.
- **Phellodon** spp.: smaller, brown spines, no tiling.
- **Young hedgehog mushrooms** (*Hydnum*): white or yellow, edible — different colour and no scales.
