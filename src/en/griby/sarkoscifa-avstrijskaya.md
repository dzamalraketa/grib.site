## Description

Fruit bodies are cups 1–5 cm, deep at first, then flatter; inner surface bright scarlet and smooth, outer paler with a fine down. On a short stem or almost none. They appear in troops on dead twigs, often half-buried in moss — giving the illusion of a red chalice floating on the ground.

## Spring beacon

The elfcup is among the first visible mushrooms of the year: in the central belt it fruits from February–March, long before the morels. For a forager it is a "tester" of the season: scarlet cups out means the soil has warmed and the forest is waking.

## The species subtlety

Until recently all scarlet elfcups were lumped as *S. coccinea*; microscopy split the complex: *austriaca* has differently curling hairs on the outer surface and spores with another oil-drop pattern. The species cannot be told apart reliably by eye — hence many guides write "the *Sarcoscypha coccinea* complex".

## Practice

Not for eating — too rare and beautiful: scarlet cups against the grey early-spring forest are better photographed. Not poisonous, but no culinary value either.
