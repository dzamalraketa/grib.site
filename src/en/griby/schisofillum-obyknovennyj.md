## Description

Cap 1–4 cm, fan- or shell-shaped, grey, felty, with an inrolled edge; **the gills are split in half lengthwise** — each half curls away on drying, protecting the spores. There is almost no stem — it sits directly on wood, often in tiers and clusters.

## Curtain gills

The "split gill" is named for its unique hymenophore: the gills are longitudinally cleft, and on drying the halves **close like curtains** over the spore surface. On wetting they reopen. A single fruit body can cycle "live-sleep-live" dozens of times.

## The most cosmopolitan fungus

*S. commune* holds the range record: every continent, every climate, every kind of wood. And the genetics-of-sex record: **over 23,000 compatible mating types** — a system so flexible that almost any pair of monospore hyphae can produce offspring.

## Practice

Inedible — tough and tiny. But a star of the mycology classroom: the cleft gills and mating genetics make it a textbook classic. In South-East Asia it is nonetheless eaten — in soups and dried.
