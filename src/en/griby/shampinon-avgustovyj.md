## Description

Cap 10–20 cm (to 30), globose then convex-flat; a white background densely overlaid with **brownish fibrillose scales** — as if dusted with cocoa. Gills free: white → pink → chocolate brown. Stem 10–20 cm, white, with a large **pendulous cogwheel ring**; below it, claw-like flakes. Flesh white, bruising **barely yellow** — and smelling of **almond/aniseed**. Spore print dark brown.

## How to rule out the toxic Agaricus

The main test is **smell plus cut colour**:

- **A. augustus**: almond/aniseed smell, cut barely yellows, brown scales on white.
- **Yellow-staining mushroom** (*A. xanthodermus*): stem base **flashes chrome yellow**, smell **phenolic/inky** — nauseating.
- **A. placomyces**: phenolic smell, yellows — inedible.
- **Young death cap**: also a white "egg" — but it has a **volva**, white gills and never a pink stage.

Almond smell + brown scales + pinking gills = the Prince confirmed.

## In the kitchen

One of the finest Agaricus for the table: dense flesh and a strong almond-anise aroma that survives frying. It dries well, pickles well and grills "as a steak" — a mature cap holds its shape. Gather young specimens with still-pink gills.

## Ecology

A saprotroph of nitrogen-rich ground: forest roads, compost edges, humus under maple and spruce. Not mycorrhizal — so it also turns up in parks and along highways. In Europe and North America it is regular but never mass-fruiting: the Prince does not grow in herds like the field mushroom.
