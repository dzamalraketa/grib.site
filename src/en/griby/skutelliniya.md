## Description

Cups 2–10 mm (usually 3–5), minute, sessile; the inner fertile surface is **bright scarlet or scarlet-orange**, the outer darker, brownish. The key trait — **dark "lashes"** at the cup margin: stiff black hairs 1–2 mm long forming a "lash fringe" — under magnification an "eye with eyelashes" appears. They grow in groups on damp wood.

## An "eye with lashes" on rotting wood

Scutellinia is a typical **microscopic** fungus: cups a few millimetres across are noticed only when you bend down to an old log. But look closely — and the "lash fringe" makes it one of the most photogenic fungi: a bright scarlet cup floor + black lash outline = an "eye" looking out of rotting timber.

## Why the "lashes"?

The dark hairs at the margin are not mere ornament: they are **stiff trichomes** holding moisture over the cup and shielding it from drying — a "microclimatic frame". Under the microscope the lashes show as septate bristles — the distinguishing mark of the genus *Scutellinia*.

## Edibility

Inedible — microscopic size and thin flesh make the question moot; its value is purely "photographic".

## Ecology

A saprotroph of very wet rotting wood — damp stumps, laid logs, moss by water, occasionally damp soil. It "follows" other decomposers: arriving on wood already softened and finishing the rest.

## Lookalikes

- **Scutellinia umbrarum/erinaceus**: other "lashed" species — separable only by bristle length and spores under the microscope.
- **Melastiza** and **Cheilymenia**: red cups but with **brown**, not black lashes.
- **Peziza**: larger, without the "lashed" rim.
