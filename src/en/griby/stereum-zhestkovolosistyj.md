## Description

Fruiting bodies are thin (1–2 mm), shelf-like or crust-like "flakes" 1–4 cm wide, often merging into long "strips" along the sides of logs. Upper side: **hairy-fuzzy, zoned — rusty, ochre, grey-brown**, with a paler margin. Underside: **smooth, no pores**, rusty brown. When cut or snapped it "bleeds" — releasing a **reddish juice** (the second trait after the hairiness). Leathery, thin.

## The "false turkey tail"

The main confusion is with [turkey tail](/en/griby/trametes-versicolor/) (*Trametes versicolor*). The distinction is simple and reliable:

| Trait | Turkey tail | Hairy curtain crust |
|---|---|---|
| Underside | **white pores** (tubes) | **smooth, no pores** |
| Upper side | velvety zones | hairy zones |
| "Bleeding" | none | **red juice when cut** |
| Flesh | leathery, elastic | thin, brittle |

**Flip it over — smooth means stereum, pores mean trametes.**

## The "bleeding" curtain

When damaged it exudes a red-orange juice — the "bleeding" reaction is typical of some stereum fungi. Not a toxin, but a chemical response to injury; visually — another "gothic" touch.

## Edibility

Inedible — thin leathery flesh of no culinary value; the role is identificational (the chief turkey-tail double) and a "winter mushroom" for observation.

## Ecology

A saprotroph — **white rot** on dead wood of broadleaf trees: branches, logs, stumps, bark crevices. Year-round, overwintering and fruiting even in frost — a "winter" species for watching the empty forest.
