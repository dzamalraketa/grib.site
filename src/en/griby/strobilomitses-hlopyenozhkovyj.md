## Description

Cap 5–15 cm, covered in large **felty grey-black scales** hanging past the edge as a ragged "collar" — the pinecone resemblance is strict. Tubes white then grey to almost black; flesh on cutting **reddens then blackens**. The stem is scaly too, netted above.

## Why "old man"

The silhouette — unkempt, grey, "bearded" — is the most recognisable form among the boletes: young it still recalls a pinecone, old it becomes a smoking black bonnet. Nothing else to confuse it with.

## Colour chemistry

The flesh on cutting **first reddens, then blackens** — the "white → red → black" sequence repeats the blackening russula. The oxidation cascade here is even prettier: the red intermediate is visible for seconds.

## Cooking

Edible — but only young, while the flesh is firm and pale; old ones turn black and sweetly unpleasant. Dried and fried like a bolete; in Poland and Eastern Europe a respected mushroom.

## Lookalikes

None — felty pinecone scaling is unique among boletes. The only similar species — *S. floccopus* — is treated as the same in our literature (the taxa are often merged).
