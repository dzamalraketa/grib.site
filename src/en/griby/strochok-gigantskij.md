## Description

Fruit body up to 15–20 cm: a brain-wrinkled yellow-brown cap atop a massive white stem — the whole resembling an overgrown morel but with a smooth cerebral surface instead of honeycomb. Flesh brittle.

## Gyromitrin — old caution vs new data

Guidebooks have long credited *G. gigas* with the same **gyromitrin** (a hydrazine hepatotoxin) as the common false morel — "in lower concentrations". But a Mycologia study (2023, 66 specimens by UHPLC) found gyromitrin is **limited** in the genus: present throughout the *G. esculenta* group and *G. leucoxantha* — and not detected in *G. gigas*. See our card `gyromitrin-distribution-mycologia-2023`.

That doesn't make it "safe": other toxins and individual reactions remain possible, and mistaking it for *G. esculenta* is deadly. The "conditional" standing stays: only after thorough boiling with the water discarded.

## Versus the common false morel

- *G. gigas*: massive white stem + yellow-brown brain-like cap, mountain conifer woods, April–June.
- *G. esculenta*: slender stem + dark brown cap, ordinary woods, March–May.
Both require boiling — esculenta is the more dangerous.

## Practice

Even the "safe" giant false morel is a gamble: gyromitrin content varies, and boiling errors cost livers. When in doubt — photograph and leave: the spring species is rare anyway.
