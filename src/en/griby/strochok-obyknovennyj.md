## Identification

The cap is reddish-brown, folded, "brain-like"; the stem whitish. False morels appear at the same time as true morels and are confused with them (see the [morel](/en/griby/smorchok/) card). The difference: a morel's cap is hollow and pitted, the false morel's is convoluted with swollen gyri.

## Why it is dangerous

The toxin is **gyromitrin**, which the body converts to monomethylhydrazine (MMH). Per StatPearls and the Michelot review (J Appl Toxicol 1991):

- gastrointestinal symptoms **5+ hours** after the meal;
- acute liver damage within two days; sometimes kidneys;
- neurotoxicity: confusion, in severe cases seizures from pyridoxal-phosphate and GABA depletion;
- human sensitivity varies, so countries classify it differently — but **processing gives no reliable safety guarantee**, and cooking vapors are toxic too.

A 2020 review (PMC7472412) recommends treating other "false morels" such as *Verpa bohemica* as inedible as well.

## In practice

Do not pick false morels and do not buy dried "morels" without exact identification. Nausea and vomiting more than 5–6 hours after spring mushrooms — call an ambulance.
