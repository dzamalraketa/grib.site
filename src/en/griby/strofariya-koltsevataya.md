## Description

Cap 5–15 cm (to 30!), bell-shaped then flat; colour **wine-red to burgundy**, fading to ochre with age. The margin carries cog-like veil remnants. Gills grey → **purple-black** (dark purplish-brown print). Stem 8–15 cm, white, with a large **cogwheel ring** — like a crown from below. Flesh white, with a pleasant mushroom smell.

## The mushroom that comes to your garden

*S. rugosoannulata* loves **woodchip and mulch**: cover a bed in chips and this mushroom is your future harvest. Hence "garden giant" — and its popularity in cultivation: spawn is sold to be laid under chip in the vegetable plot. In nature it is the same wood-litter saprotroph: parks, edges, compost.

## In the kitchen — with a caveat

Edible and even tasty: firm, nutty flesh — it pickles, fries and dries well. **But** gather young specimens — over-mature ones turn loose and can cause upset. And the main rule of the urban edible: **do not eat mushrooms off someone else's mulch**, where they may have soaked up treated chemicals (see our metal-accumulation cards).

## Lookalikes

- **Other Stropharia** (*S. aeruginosa*, *S. caerulea*): blue-green, woodland, no wine colour.
- **Agaricus**: white print + pink → brown gills; Stropharia is dark from the start.
- **Psathyrellas**: smaller, no ring and no burgundy.

## Ecology

A saprotroph on woodchip, straw and compost — humans prepare its substrate for it. A cosmopolitan of the temperate belt; in Russia — a regular "garden stray" in cultivated landscapes. One of the few mushrooms you can summon at will: buy spawn, cover with chip, harvest.
