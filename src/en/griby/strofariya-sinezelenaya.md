## Description

Cap 3–8 cm, at first **intensely blue-green** (copper-sulphate blue), slimy, with white woolly flecks at the rim; fading to yellow-green with age. Gills white then smoky violet to nearly black (dark violet-brown spores). Stem scaly, with a ring and slime below it.

## The "chemical" colour as signal

The blue-green pigment is one of the rarest in the fungal world; it likely works as a warning. The species is considered **mildly poisonous**: GI upsets are recorded, and some sources report possible psychoactive effects — so the card says "poisonous", not merely "inedible".

## Ecology

A saprotroph on varied organic substrates: leaf litter, wood chips, garden soil, dung. One of those fungi that liven up the autumn forest after the chanterelles — peak fruiting September–October.

## Lookalikes

Almost nothing to confuse: slimy copper-green cap + white woolly rim + dark spores is a unique combination. Fading old fruit bodies recall other stropharias — likewise inedible/poisonous.
