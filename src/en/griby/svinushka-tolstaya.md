## Description

Cap 8–25 cm, semicircular then funnel-shaped; surface dry, velvety, dark brown; margin inrolled. Gills crowded, yellow, bruising dark brown. Stem short, thick, eccentric, dark and **velvety-black** — the key mark. Flesh yellowish, firm, with a sourish smell.

## Where it grows — the main distinction

The velvet roll-rim is a **wood** species: stumps, deadfall, dead conifer roots. The brown roll-rim (*Paxillus involutus*) grows on soil in troops under birch and conifers. A "roll-rim" on a stump is almost certainly this species.

## Toxicity

Contains the pigment **atromentin** (an anticoagulant) and related terphenylquinones; causes severe gastrointestinal poisoning. Although older Soviet books listed it as "edible after soaking", modern sources place it firmly among the poisonous:
- High levels of toxic metabolites not destroyed by cooking.
- Possible cumulative toxicity, as in the brown roll-rim — though no immune mechanism has been proven for this species.

## Practice

Do not pick. The large velvety caps on stumps look attractive, but the species belongs to the same risk group as the brown roll-rim: delayed toxicity and unpredictable individual reactions.
