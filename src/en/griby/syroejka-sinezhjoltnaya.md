## Description

Cap 5–15 cm, convex then flattened to depressed; colour variegated — purple, lilac, greenish, olive with yellow and blue zones, often with an oily sheen. Gills white to cream, **flexible and greasy to the touch** — they bend rather than crumble, unlike the brittle gills of other russulas. Stem white, sometimes purple-flushed. Flesh white, firm, sweetish.

## Field mark — the "greasy" gills

*R. cyanoxantha* differs from other russulas in its gill tissue: abundant sphaerocysts make the gills bend under pressure instead of crumbling, feeling like thin greasy card. If a "russula" has gills that won't snap, it is almost certainly cyanoxantha.

## Cooking

Rated the best edible russula: the firm flesh holds together when fried and has a sweet, nutty flavour. Good fried, stewed or salted; unlike acrid russulas it needs no soaking. A popular market mushroom in the Mediterranean.

## Lookalikes

No poisonous russula shares the "greasy" gill character — the mark is reliable. Care is only needed against confusion with acrid russulas (*R. fragilis*, *R. emetica*), but those have brittle gills and bitter flesh — the hot taste warns you anyway.
