## Description

Shelves 2–10 cm, semicircular, thin (2–4 mm), often merging into long rows or tiling. Upper side: **coarsely hairy to shaggy**, greyish, dirty brown or ochre, with weak zones; old ones green with algae. Underside: **cream-grey or brownish round pores** (2–4 per mm). Flesh thin, corky, two-layered. Fruit bodies are annual but persist to the next season — seeming "eternal".

## Turkey tail's nearest kin

Hirsuta is the sister of [turkey tail](/en/griby/trametes-versicolor/): same form, same ecology. Three traits tell them apart:

| Trait | Trametes versicolor | Trametes hirsuta |
|---|---|---|
| Upper side | velvety **vivid** zones | **coarsely hairy**, dull grey-brown tones |
| Pores | white, fine | greyish-brown, coarser |
| Flesh | uniform | two-layered |

In the field the "turkey tail" is rainbow-zoned — hirsuta is duller and "hairier".

## Ecology

A saprotroph — **white rot** on dead broadleaf wood: birch, beech, alder, willow. Branches, logs, stumps, firewood; often the first colonist of fresh-fallen wood. Year-round.

## Edibility

Inedible — tough corky flesh; same status as its relative: "look, don't eat".

## Lookalikes

- **Turkey tail**: vivid zones, fine white pores, softer.
- **Hairy curtain crust**: thinner, **no pores below**, "bleeds".
- **Trametes pubescens**: smaller, softer, creamier.
