## Description

Fruit body — a shapeless **jelly "cerebral" mass** 2–10 cm, bright yellow or orange, lobed; in dry weather it shrinks to a crust, in wet it swells back into a "brain". Sits on dead broadleaf branches, sometimes seeming to "pour" out of the bark.

## A fungus-parasite of fungus

The main secret of tremella: it does **not decompose wood itself**. Its mycelium parasitises the mycelium of **Peniophora** — a crust fungus living under the bark. So a "yellow brain" is always a sign of a hidden peniophora: find the tremella, and somewhere nearby its host is at work.

## Ecology — the winter lodger

It fruits September–March — shrugging off light frosts and thaws; the jelly consistency is itself antifreeze. In mid-winter the "yellow brain" is among the few bright finds of the bare forest.

## Practice

Inedible — a jelly mass without taste or structure. But interesting as the **third level of parasitism** in the catalogue: the slimy spike and copper spike on other fungi's mycorrhiza, and the tremella — directly on the fungus itself. Mycology is layers.
