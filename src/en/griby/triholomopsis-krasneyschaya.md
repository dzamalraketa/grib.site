## Description

Cap 5–15 cm, **purple-red in fine scales on a yellow ground** — that very combination gave the English name "plums and custard": plum-purple scales on a custard-yellow field. Gills bright yellow, adnate. Stem yellow with purple scales at the base.

## The "dessert" livery

The contrast of purple scales on a yellow field is unique in our mycobiota: no other common mushroom wears both colours at once. Photographers prize it: "plums in custard" is the most Instagrammable autumn mushroom.

## Ecology

A saprotroph on dead conifer wood — stumps, deadfall, buried branches. Usually pine/spruce; singly or in small groups. In the central belt a regular autumn species, though never massed.

## Practice

Inedible — the flesh is astringent-bitter. There is nothing to gather: the mushroom is beautiful precisely on the wood, where it should remain. In the catalogue — as one of the loveliest "inedibles" and a good teaching case that "mushroom ≠ only food".
