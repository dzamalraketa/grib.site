## Description

Cap 10–40 cm, fan- or kidney-shaped; creamy yellow with dark brown scales laid concentrically — "pheasant's feathers". Pores large, angular, running down the eccentric stem. Young flesh firm and juicy with a characteristic **watermelon-rind/cucumber** smell; old flesh tough and "leathery".

## A spring mushroom

One of the year's first edible fungi — fruiting from May, when morels fade and summer boletes are still absent. Visible from afar: large cream-and-brown "shelves" on stumps and trunks.

## Cooking — young only

Edible strictly when young: while the cap is soft and the stem yields to a knife, the mushroom can be fried or stewed; the watermelon-cucumber aroma cooks off. Mature specimens are indigestibly tough — leave them.

## Ecology

A saprotroph and weak parasite of dead/weakened hardwood — one of the decomposers of elm and maple trunks. In the forest it works as a recycler-sanitizer.
