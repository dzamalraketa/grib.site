## Description

Subterranean fruit body 3–10 cm, rounded or irregular, black-brown with a warty surface. The gleba is dark, marbled with white veins — the spore-bearing asci. The aroma is deep, earthy-musky with nut and cocoa notes, built on sulfides and volatile terpenes — exactly what trained dogs detect.

## Why it is expensive

- **Harvest**: an underground fungus that cannot be found without a dog (or trained pig); gathered in winter when other foraging is closed.
- **Cultivation**: inoculated oak and hazel seedlings are sold widely, but a new plantation takes 7–10 years to crop, and roughly half never produce.
- **Habitat limits**: requires calcareous soils and a Mediterranean climate; recent harvests suffer from heat and drought.

## Biology

The *T. melanosporum* genome was fully sequenced in 2010 (Nature, PMID 20348908) — a landmark in symbiosis research: the fungus secretes enzymes that reshape the host's root architecture and switches the root into a "truffle programme" without entering plant cells.

## Versus the summer truffle

|Tuber melanosporum|Tuber aestivum|
|---|---|
|Season: November — March|Season: summer — autumn|
|Powerful, "royal" aroma|Weaker aroma|
|Price €1–3k/kg|Price €200–500/kg|
|Dark purple flesh|Lighter flesh|

## Cooking

A classic of French cuisine: thin slices over hot dishes so the fat captures the volatiles; heat briefly or the bouquet is lost. Infuses oil, rice, eggs, pasta beautifully. Beware Chinese *T. indicum* — visually similar, aromatically empty, and sometimes mixed in fraudulently.
