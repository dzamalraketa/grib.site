## Description

Fruit body — a **cup 4–10 mm** tall, conical to bell-shaped, fluted inside (hence "striatus"); inside sit 3–5 greyish-brown "eggs" — **peridioles**, spore-filled packets. Outside the cup is brownish, shaggy; inside — smooth, with longitudinal ribs.

## The "eggs in a nest" mechanism

It is no quirk — it is a **catapult**: a raindrop falls into the cup, its splash catches the peridioles and hurls them 1–1.5 metres. The "eggs" snag on grass with sticky threads (funiculi) and germinate there. One of the most elegant spore-dispersal mechanisms in nature — pure ballistics.

## Ecology

A saprotroph on dead wood chips, mulch, fallen twigs and rich soil — common in gardens and parks wherever wood chip is used. Appears in June and fruits until the frosts.

## Practice

Inedible — and far too small to eat. But for children (and adults) it is the perfect "find of the day": a "nest with eggs" under a hand lens is worth any walk.
