## Description

Fruiting bodies 3–9 cm tall, **deep cup- or vase-shaped "urns"**: the inner surface smooth, **black or dark brown**, the outer grey, slightly fuzzy. A "vase" shape — a narrow base on a short stem-like foot widening upward, the rim sometimes fringed. Flesh thin, leathery-elastic, grey. Appears in early spring, often in clusters of several "goblets" — like an exhibition of black vases on the leaf litter.

## The "devil's urn"

The English "devil's urn" says it all: dark "goblets" on a grey foot standing on last year's leaves at the foot of oaks and maples. Colour — grey to blue-black. Mature cups "puff" spore dust — visible as smoke if you blow on one.

## Edibility

Inedible — the flesh is leathery and thin, of no culinary value; the value is the **find itself**: a rare, beautiful spring sight.

## Ecology

A saprotroph: soil near fallen logs, the root zone of broadleaf trees — an ascomycete that works the "decomposed" end of wood. One of spring's first fungi: fruiting April–June, often at the same spot for years.

## Lookalikes

- **Dark brown pezizas** (*Pseudoplectania* and others): dark cups, but smaller and on wood.
- **Melastiza**: tiny black cups, different form.
- **Bulgaria inquinans**: black "saucers", but on branches and slimy outside.
