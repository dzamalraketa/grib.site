## Description

Cap 3–10 cm, convex → nearly flat, **dull ochre, yellow-brown or brick with a paler ("baked") rim** — hence "pie" in the English names. Surface slightly sticky. Gills crowded, adnate, cream → **brown**, exuding **watery droplets** when mature that dry to brown spots. Stem pale, whitish-pruinose toward the top, mealy at the apex. Spore print **tobacco-brown**.

## The radish smell

The field "password" of the genus *Hebeloma* is a **distinct smell of radish** (in some species, cocoa with radish). Crush a gill — and the smell gives the hebeloma away at once. Add the characteristic **droplet "tears"** on the gills of young fruit bodies — guttation that later leaves rusty dots.

## Toxicity

Poisonous: a **gastrointestinal syndrome** — nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, abdominal pain 30 minutes to a couple of hours after eating. Usually resolves in 1–2 days, but unpleasant. One of the most common "accidental" mushrooms in poisonings — abundant, ordinary near human habitation, edible-looking. **Do not pick it.**

## Ecology

Mycorrhizal with a broad range of trees (birch, oak, beech, pine, spruce, willow), which explains its ubiquity: it grows everywhere — in the woods, in the park, on the lawn by the house, at forest edge, by the road. Often fruits in groups and **fairy rings**.

## Lookalikes

- **Russulas**: brittle flesh, no radish smell, no gill droplets.
- **Tricholomas**: usually different colour, gills without "tears" and no browning print.
- **Other hebelomas** (H. sinapizans etc.): similar pattern; the whole genus *Hebeloma* is considered suspect — the rule is "do not eat hebelomas".
