## Description

The "cap" is 1.5–4 cm, **wrinkled, folded, thimble- or bell-shaped**, light to dark brown, **attached to the stem only at the very top** — the edges hang free, as if a thimble were "slipped" over the stem (the key trait of *Verpa*: the cap attaches only at its centre). Stem long (up to 10 cm), cream, **cottony-hollow inside**, often longitudinally grooved. Appears in early spring alongside the snowdrops.

## How it differs from a true morel

| Trait | Bohemian verpa | True morel |
|---|---|---|
| Cap attachment | **only the apex** | fully fused to the stem |
| Shape | thimble, smooth folds | pitted "honeycomb" |
| Stem | cottony-hollow | hollow, brittle |

**Slice lengthwise**: in a morel cap and stem are one; in a verpa the thimble comes off — a "cap on a stick".

## Edibility: caution

Traditionally gathered as a spring mushroom: **long boiling (20+ minutes) with the broth discarded**. However *Verpa bohemica* carries trace amounts of **gyromitrin** (the same toxin as in false morels!) — in some people it causes poisoning: dizziness, loss of coordination, nausea. Some countries officially list it as **inedible** because of gyromitrin. A sensible stance: "conditionally edible" — better to choose a true morel.

## Ecology

A soil saprotroph: broadleaf woods, edges, river bottoms, nettle patches — under aspens, poplars, maple. One of the **earliest mushrooms of the year** — fruiting in April–May, sometimes literally through last year's leaves.

## Lookalikes

- **True morels** (*Morchella*): pitted cap fused to the stem along its whole length.
- **False morels** (*Gyromitra*): larger, brain-like, heavily gyromitrin-laden — **dangerous** if mishandled.
- **Half-free morel** (*Mitrophora*): cap half-fused to the stem.
