## Description

Cap 4–10 cm, fan- or shell-shaped, light grey to nearly white ("pulmonarius" from its airy pallor, not lungs); surface smooth, margin inrolled at first. Gills white, decurrent down a short lateral stem or almost none. Fruits in overlapping "tiles" on wood.

## Versus the common oyster

- Season: **May–September** versus autumn for *P. ostreatus*.
- Colour: paler — white to pale grey.
- Cap: thinner and more delicate, margin wavier.
- Stem: often shorter or absent — fruit bodies "sit" directly on wood.

## Cooking

A full-value oyster: frying, soups, mushroom patties, drying. Young fruit bodies are more tender than old — pick while the edge is still inrolled. Like all oysters it contains **lovastatin-like** compounds and beta-glucans — the basis of several of our research cards.

## Cultivation

The genus's second cultivated species after *P. ostreatus*; used in home mushroom growing on logs, straw and sawdust — faster and more warmth-loving than the common oyster.
