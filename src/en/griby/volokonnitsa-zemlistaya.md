## Description

Cap 1–3 cm, bell-shaped → flattened with a characteristic **umbo**; colour white to cream-ochre, surface **silky-fibrous**, cracking with age. Gills whitish → brown. Stem 2–5 cm, slender, white, ringless. Spore print **brown** (tobacco-brown — the key genus character). Smell earthy, faintly spicy.

## Muscarine in "innocent" white

*I. geophylla* contains **muscarine** — the same neurotoxin as in our fool's funnel card. Symptoms — the same muscarinic syndrome (sweating, tearing, bradycardia, GI) within 15–30 minutes; antidote atropine. Its deceptively "innocent" look — white, small, "lawn" — makes it a frequent accidental pick by children and novice foragers.

## A third muscarine card — the "white wave"

We already have muscarine cards: the fool's funnel (meadows) and part of the blushing fibercap. *I. geophylla* is **mycorrhizal** and woodland: it completes the muscarine picture as the most **abundant** white LBM with confirmed muscarine. As with the funnel, the mechanism is toxin release on tissue destruction (`muscarine-release-mechanism-2024` — the same phosphorylated precursor is found in Inocybe!).

## Identification points

White cap + umbo + silky cap + **brown print** (the key — not white!) + earthy smell = fibercap. The rule "never touch small white LBMs" — fibercap, Lepiota cristata and the fool's funnel make it literally a survival rule.

## Lookalikes

- **Inocybe erubescens** (blushing fibercap, in our catalogue): reddens where damaged.
- **Inocybe rimosa** and other "brown fibercaps": brown, not white — same muscarine.
- **White Mycena/Lepiota**: no brown print and no umbo "nipple".

## Ecology

Mycorrhizal with a wide host range — oak, beech, pine, spruce: one of the most **undemanding** mycorrhizal fungi, hence its abundance — from old woodland to gardens and parks.
