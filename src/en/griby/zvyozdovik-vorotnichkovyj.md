## Description

The young fruit body is a 3–5 cm puffball-like bulb half-buried in soil. Then the outer skin (exoperidium) cracks and opens into 4–8 ray-"petals" curling back; on them sits a round spore sac with a pore on top — the whole structure is the "earthstar". In *triplex* the rays often fold under the sac, forming a "collar".

## How the "shot" works

Spores inside the sac ripen to dust; the apical pore is the nozzle. Each raindrop or twig-strike squeezes the sac — and it ejects a puff of spores. A fungus adapted to point dispersal: the same mechanism as puffballs, but in architecturally finer packaging.

## The genus Geastrum

Dozens of earthstar species (collared, sessile, elegant), differing in ray count and sac shape. All inedible and harmless — a purely contemplative group.

## Practice

Finding an earthstar is a photo occasion: they are beautiful precisely when opened and persist for weeks without decaying, so the "star" can be shown off long after discovery. Inedible — there is virtually no flesh to eat.
