Enjalbert F., Rapior S., Nouguier-Soulé J., Guillon S., Amouroux N., Cabot C. Treatment of amatoxin poisoning: 20-year retrospective analysis. J Toxicol Clin Toxicol. 2002;40(6):715-57.

## Rationale

Amatoxin poisoning is the deadliest mushroom intoxication: a long latent phase (6–24 h), gastroenteritis, a deceptive "recovery", then hepatotoxicity, liver failure and coma. The toxins — α-, β-, γ-amanitins — are bicyclic octapeptides that irreversibly block RNA polymerase II. By 2002 no prospective RCT of therapy existed — and likely never will: cases are rare and scattered.

## Methods

All published clinical data on hospitalised amatoxin poisonings in North America and Europe over ~20 years — **2,108 cases**. Supportive measures and 10 specific treatment regimens (detoxification: forced diuresis, biliary drainage, extracorporeal purification; chemotherapy: benzylpenicillin and other β-lactams, silymarin, thioctic acid, antioxidants, hormones and steroids) were compared against mortality by chi-square analysis.

## Results

- **Benzylpenicillin (penicillin G)** was the most used drug — but showed **little efficacy**.
- **Thioctic acid and steroids** — no benefit.
- **Silybin** (intravenous, alone or combined) and **N-acetylcysteine** (monotherapy) — the only regimens statistically associated with survival.
- 35 amatoxin-containing species across three genera: *Amanita*, *Galerina*, *Lepiota* — "little brown mushrooms" are as dangerous as the death cap.

## Authors' conclusions

The best candidates for standard therapy are silybin and NAC plus aggressive supportive care; prospective studies should confirm efficacy.

## Worth noting

Enjalbert's analysis is the de facto foundation of the modern protocol: afterwards, intravenous silibinin (Legalon SIL in Europe, IND in the US) plus NAC became the standard. Twenty years on, no prospective RCT has appeared — rarity makes one nearly impossible. Retrospective design is biased: the sickest patients received everything at once, so the "combination effect" may be an artefact. But no dataset of this scale exists or is likely to.
