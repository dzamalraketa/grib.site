Falandysz J. et al. Distribution of mercury in Gypsy Cortinarius caperatus mushrooms from several populations: an efficient accumulator species. Ecotoxicol Environ Saf. 2014;110:68-72.

## Why this study

Our gypsy card is the only "brave" webcap — but edible ≠ clean: *C. caperatus* is known as one of the best **mercury accumulators** among fungi. This field study checks that on real populations.

## Methods

- Specimens from **20 spatially distant sites** in Poland — forested, background.
- Hg analysis separately in caps and stems; bioconcentration factor (BCF) calculated.

## Findings

- Median cap Hg: **0.81–2.4 mg/kg** dry weight (stems 2.5× lower).
- Cap BCF: **18–120** — the mushroom concentrates mercury far above its soil.
- **Even in clean forest sites** the concentration exceeded background — a property of the species, not only pollution.
- Occasional consumption — safe; **frequent season-long eating — not recommended**.

## Why it matters

The lesson: "edible" and "safe for frequent eating" are different statuses. The gypsy is an honest edible — but with an accumulative trait. The practical note now on our card: gather moderately, not "bagfuls all season", and don't make it a dietary staple.
