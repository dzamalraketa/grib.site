J Fungi (Basel) 2019. Influence of the Nutrients on the Biomass and Pigment Production of Chlorociboria aeruginascens (PMID 31100858).

## Why this study

In our elfcup card: "green timber" is the fungus's trace, prized in marquetry. But the pigment need not only be found — it can be **grown**: this work is about laboratory xylindein production for veneers and even organic semiconductors.

## What was studied

- The effect of **carbon and nitrogen sources** on biomass growth and xylindein synthesis.
- Separation of two phases: primary metabolism (colourless biomass growth) and secondary (pigment production).

## Findings

- **Pigment is decoupled from growth**: colourless biomass forms in primary metabolism, xylindein in secondary — untied from cell division.
- Best nitrogen — **yeast extract** (complex organic); carbohydrates varied, but the carbon/nitrogen ratio was critical for yield.
- By controlling the medium one can **programme** production: first "feed" the fungus, then "switch on" the pigment.

## Why it matters

- Xylindein is not just "pretty wood" but a **functional material**: studied for veneer coatings and organic semiconductors — a natural pigment replacing synthetic dyes.
- It shows the "tamability" of fungal pigments: what in the forest is a turquoise smudge on a stump becomes a controllable bio-factory in the lab.
- Links to our dye species (Sarcodon, Phaeolus): natural fungal pigments are a growing field between mycology and materials science.
