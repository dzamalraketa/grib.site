Wieland T., Enderlein M. Taxonomy and toxicity of Conocybe lactea and related species. Mycol Res. 2003;107(8):951-956.

## Why this study

Continuation of our "toxin mapping" line (gyromitrin across Discinaceae → amatoxins across Lepiota). Here the question is different: **do phallotoxins exist outside Amanita**? Until this paper phalloid toxin was thought an amanita monopoly.

## Methods

- Specimens of *Conocybe lactea* — a common local conecap; for comparison — C. filaris (our "wrinkled conecap").
- **HPLC + mass spectrometry** for amatoxins and phallotoxins.
- Phylogeny on nuclear rRNA genes — to see whether the toxic species are neighbours.

## Findings

- In *C. lactea* there are **no amatoxins** — but **phallotoxins in small quantities**: the first phalloid toxins found outside the genus Amanita.
- *C. filaris* (= *Pholiotina rugosa*) — a confirmed bearer of **amatoxins**.
- Phylogeny showed *C. lactea* and *C. filaris* are **not close relatives**; the toxins evolved independently.

## Why it matters

The result mirrors the Lepiota work: the toxinology map of fungi is more complex than the genus scheme. Phallotoxins outside Amanita — previously thought impossible; amatoxin in a "harmless" LBM — likewise. It confirms the catalogue rule: little brown mushrooms (LBMs) are not a category of "unpalatable" but of "undetermined".
