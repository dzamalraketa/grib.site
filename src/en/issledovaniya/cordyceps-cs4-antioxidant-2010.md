Sci Rep 2024. A randomized controlled clinical trial examining the effects of Cordyceps militaris beverage on the immune response in healthy adults (PMID 38580687).

## Rationale

Cordyceps militaris products are popular as "immune boosters", yet human clinical data were scarce. Researchers in Phayao (Thailand) developed a submerged-fermentation functional beverage (FCM) and tested its effects on immune markers and safety in healthy volunteers.

## Methods

Randomized placebo-controlled trial: 20 healthy adults (10 men, 10 women) received FCM containing 2.85 mg cordycepin or a placebo daily for 8 weeks. At baseline, week 4 and week 8 they measured immune cell markers (NK-cell activity), immunoglobulins, cytokines and standard safety panels (hepatic, renal, hematological, glucose, lipids).

## Results

- NK-cell activity **increased significantly** in men by week 4 on FCM (p=0.049) and in women by week 8 (p=0.023) versus baseline.
- Within-group analysis: **IL-1β decreased in men** (p=0.049), **IL-6 decreased in women** (p=0.047).
- Glucose, lipid profile and safety indices **did not differ** between groups — no liver, kidney or blood toxicity was detected.

## Conclusions

The authors conclude that FCM is a candidate immune-boosting supplement without hepato-, nephro- or hematotoxicity. The key functional signal is increased NK-cell activity (natural killers — key effectors of antiviral immunity) with different dynamics in men and women.

## Caveats

- **Tiny sample** (n=20, 10 per sex): the effect needs confirmation in larger RCTs; the sex differences may be chance at this n.
- **Surrogate markers** measured (NK activity, cytokines), not clinical outcomes (URI frequency, severity) — a link to real "cold resistance" is unproven.
- The product is a **submerged-fermentation beverage** with a known cordycepin dose (2.85 mg), not fruiting bodies or an arbitrary powder: results cannot be extrapolated to any "cordyceps supplement".
