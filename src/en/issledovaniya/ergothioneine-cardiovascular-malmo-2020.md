Smith E. et al. Ergothioneine is associated with reduced mortality and decreased risk of cardiovascular disease. Heart. 2020;106(9):691-697.

## Why this study

Ergothioneine is the "mushroom antioxidant" the body hoards via a dedicated transporter, OCTN1. But before this paper no large human cohort had tested whether blood ergothioneine **predicts real outcomes** rather than just "health benefit" in vitro.

## Methods

- Prospective **Malmö Diet and Cancer** cohort: 3236 participants free of CVD and diabetes at baseline.
- **112 plasma metabolites** measured by LC-MS; searched for metabolites tied to the "health conscious food pattern" (HCFP).
- Median follow-up — **21.4 years**; endpoints: coronary disease, diabetes, cardiovascular and all-cause mortality.

## Findings

Ergothioneine was the metabolite **most strongly connected to the healthy-eating pattern** — and predicted outcomes (HR per 1 SD increment):
- **Coronary disease**: HR 0.85 (−15% risk)
- **Cardiovascular mortality**: HR 0.79 (−21%)
- **All-cause mortality**: HR 0.86 (−14%)

## Limitations

- Observational — association is not causation; ergothioneine may be a marker of an overall "mushroom/fish/vegetable" diet.
- The metabolite was measured once at baseline.

## Why it matters

This is the **fourth branch** of ergothioneine on the site: cancer (Ba meta-analysis), MCI (Feng), plasma prediction of cognitive decline (Wu) — and now mortality/CVD (Smith). Malmö's uniqueness — the longest follow-up and largest n of all our ergothioneine cards.
