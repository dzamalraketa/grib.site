Phytochemistry 2026. Naphthalene derivatives and other constituents with immunomodulatory activities from the fruiting bodies of Fomes fomentarius (PMID 42849829).

## Rationale

The tinder polypore (amadou) is one of the oldest "medicinal" fungi: it was used as a hemostatic dressing in antiquity (and as tinder for fire). Yet the species' chemistry remains fragmentarily studied. The task set: what exactly is inside the fruiting bodies, and do any components show measurable bioactivity?

## Methods

- Chemical investigation of F. fomentarius fruiting bodies: compound isolation.
- Structural elucidation: NMR, HRMS, J-based configuration analysis (JBCA) and computational methods.
- In vitro bioevaluation: CCK-8, EdU and ELISA assays on stimulated T and B lymphocytes.

## Results

- **11 compounds** isolated, including two previously undescribed — **fominols A and B** bearing a rare 7,10-epoxycyclohepta[de]naphthalene skeleton.
- Compounds 9 and 11 showed **moderate antiproliferative effects** on stimulated T/B lymphocytes along with reduced secretion — potential immunomodulatory activity.

## Conclusions

The authors describe a new class of natural naphthalene derivatives from the tinder polypore and record moderate immunomodulatory activity of individual components on cells.

## Caveats

- This is **in vitro chemistry**, not clinical data: "immunomodulation on a cell culture" cannot be extrapolated to human use. There are no PubMed-indexed clinical studies of F. fomentarius yet — the card honestly marks the level as "interesting chemistry".
- The work's value is **foundational**: it maps the chemical inventory of a species whose traditional reputation ("the hemostatic fungus") outpaces its study.
- Direct comparison with the catalog's "medicinal" mushrooms is inappropriate: cordyceps and lion's mane have human RCTs; the tinder polypore has isolated molecules and cell cultures — different floors of evidence.
