Briggs et al. Not all bad: Gyromitrin has a limited distribution in the false morels as determined by a new ultra high-performance liquid chromatography method. Mycologia. 2023;115(1):38-53.

## Why this study

Every guidebook says "false morels contain gyromitrin" — as if the toxin ran through the whole genus. Nobody had tested it broadly. This paper developed a sensitive UHPLC method and **mapped gyromitrin across 66 specimens** of Discinaceae.

## Methods

- New analytical method: 2,4-dinitrobenzaldehyde derivatization + UHPLC.
- **66 specimens / 105 tests** across Discinaceae (Gyromitra, Hydnotrya etc.).
- ITS + 28S sequencing to confirm species and build a phylogeny.

## Findings

- Gyromitrin was detected **in all specimens of the *G. esculenta* group** and in *G. leucoxantha*.
- In the rest of the genus — **not detected** (or below sensitivity): a *limited*, not genus-wide, distribution.
- Phylogeny fits **rapid evolution + horizontal transfer** of biosynthesis — typical of secondary metabolites.

## Why it matters

This work recalibrates our gyromitra cards: "contains gyromitrin, but less" for *G. gigas* is probably **outdated caution** — the study found no gyromitrin in giant false morel specimens. That doesn't make it "safe" (other toxins, individual reactions, rarity of harvest), but describes its real chemistry more accurately.
