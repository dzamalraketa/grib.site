Toxins 2020. Warning on false or true morels and button mushrooms with potential toxicity linked to hydrazinic toxins: an update. Lagrange E, Vernoux JP (PMID 32751277).

## Why this study

Our new [Bohemian verpa card](/en/griby/smorchkovaya-shapochka/) mentions trace gyromitrin — this review supplies the direct rationale for caution: its authors (a neurologist and a toxicologist) regard *Verpa bohemica* as **inedible**, and show that "traditional cooking" does not zero out risk across the morel-like group.

## What the review covers

- **Gyromitrin** — the hydrazinic toxin of *Gyromitra esculenta*: in the body it converts to monomethylhydrazine, a neuro- and hepatotoxin, carcinogenic in animals. The disputed "edibility" of false morels is explained by **individual susceptibility**.
- **Verpa bohemica**: the authors propose counting it **inedible** too — gyromitrin is present, and thorough cooking is unproven as full detoxification.
- **True morels** (*Morchella*): heavy consumption has produced a **transient neurological syndrome with cerebellar signs** (impaired coordination), and raw or undercooked morels — a **gastrointestinal "haemolytic" syndrome**.
- **Agaritine in button mushrooms** (*Agaricus*): a water-soluble hydrazine related to gyromitrin; a weak potential carcinogen in mice; no direct human data, with an estimated lifetime extra cancer risk around 10⁻⁵ — well below many everyday risks.

## Why it matters

- **"Traditionally eaten" ≠ "safe"**: boiling reduces but does not guarantee gyromitrin detox — and human susceptibility varies.
- Direct support for our verpa's cautious `conditional` status: not a "poor man's morel" but a mushroom carrying a real toxin.
- A measured contrast with the button mushroom: the agaritine risk is estimated at ~10⁻⁵ — **far lower** than the risk from gyromitrin-bearing species.

## Limitations

- **A review**, not new research: conclusions are assembled from heterogeneous clinical and analytical sources.
- For *Verpa bohemica*, quantitative gyromitrin data are sparse — "inedible" here is a conservative assessment rather than a dose-derived figure.
- The agaritine cancer risk is a mouse extrapolation; no direct epidemiological data exist for humans.
