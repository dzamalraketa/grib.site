Michigan Poison & Drug Information Center. A 19-year longitudinal assessment of gyromitrin-containing (Gyromitra spp.) mushroom poisonings in Michigan. Toxicon. 2024.

## Why this study

False morels (*Gyromitra*) are among the few mushrooms treated as an edible delicacy in some countries and as deadly in others. Textbook teaching: gyromitrin → monomethylhydrazine → seizures. This paper checks the classics against real data: **19 years** of reports to a major poison center.

## Methods

Retrospective review of all cases logged by the Michigan Poison & Drug Information Center from Jan 1, 2002 to Dec 31, 2020: 118 reports involving known or suspected gyromitrin-containing mushrooms.

## Findings

- **91.5%** (108/118) were *G. esculenta*, the common false morel.
- Dominant picture — **gastrointestinal + hepatotoxicity**, not the textbook neurotoxic seizures.
- Root cause of poisonings — **misidentification**: false morels taken for edible *Morchella*.
- Mechanism: gyromitrin metabolizes to **monomethylhydrazine** — the rocket-fuel hepatotoxin.

## Why it matters for our card

Our giant false morel card keeps "conditional" status with caveats — and this series shows why: even "safer" gyromitras appear rarely in reports mainly because they are rarely eaten. Nineteen years of real poisonings show that identification error, not "bad boiling", is the main mechanism.
