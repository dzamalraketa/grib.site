Sci Rep 2020. Toxic metabolite profiling of Inocybe virosa (PMID 32792538).

## Why this study

Our new white fibercap card describes an "innocent-looking" white mushroom carrying muscarine. This work gives direct **chemical confirmation**: the toxin in fibercaps is instrumentally measured, not inferred from symptoms.

## Methods

- **Gas chromatography** of volatile metabolites of *Inocybe virosa*.
- **Liquid chromatography** — direct detection of muscarine.
- In vitro bioactivity tests of the isolated compounds.

## Findings

- **Muscarine confirmed by LC** — the same neurotoxin as in our fool's funnel and white fibercap cards.
- Volatile toxic metabolites: **methyl palmitate, phenolic compounds, phytol** — additional "chemical markers" of the toxic species.
- Morphological similarity of edible and poisonous Inocybe is the reason only chemistry can tell a "safe" species apart with certainty.

## Why it matters

- **"White and small" ≠ "safe"**: chemical profiling confirms muscarine sits precisely in the "innocent" white caps.
- The work extends our "toxin-mapping" line (Phytochemistry-2022 for Lepiota, UHPLC-2023 for gyromitrin): now Inocybe too has **direct** chromatographic confirmation of muscarine.
- The practical conclusion for our fibercap card: "do not taste" — there is a measured toxin inside, not a "mild threat".
