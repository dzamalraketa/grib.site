Chu K.K.W. et al. Herb-drug interactions between the medicinal mushrooms Lingzhi and Yunzhi and cytotoxic anticancer drugs: a systematic review. Chin Med. 2020;15:75.

## Why this study

Patients on chemotherapy often add "immune" mushrooms — reishi (Lingzhi) and yunzhi (Trametes versicolor/PSP-PSK). How their components interact with cytotoxics had barely been studied. This review is the first attempt to systematise all pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic data.

## Methods

- Search of **9 Chinese and English databases**: clinical, animal and in-vitro work on co-administration with cytotoxics.
- **213 studies** found, **77 clinical** (56 yunzhi, 21 lingzhi).
- Clinical quality scored on the Jadad scale.

## Findings

- **Pharmacokinetic interactions** are barely documented: only 2 clinical PK studies — no significant effect on cytotoxic metabolism found.
- **Commonest combinations**: with lingzhi — cisplatin, 5-FU, paclitaxel; with yunzhi — tegafur/UFT, 5-FU, mitomycin.
- **Clinical study quality is low** (per Jadad); no hard contraindications were established either.

## Limitations

- Few human PK studies — cannot exclude simply unstudied interactions.
- Extracts and doses vary — "reishi" means different preparations across authors.

## Why it matters

The practical takeaway: **don't reflexively add medicinal mushrooms to chemotherapy** — but nor is there evidence for blanket prohibition. The review frames all our reishi/turkey-tail cards: "probably safe, evidence modest, talk to your oncologist."
