Krakowska A. et al. Decoding trace element speciation in mushrooms: Analytical techniques, comprehensive data review, and health implications. Food Chem. 2025;463:141160.

## Why this study

We used to say "mushrooms accumulate metals" in general terms. This review is the first to break down **speciation** — which chemical form of each element — across five key trace elements: As, Cr, Se, Hg, Sb. The form decides the toxicity — and the review shows how much we still don't know.

## Methods

- Systematic review of the literature on trace-element speciation in edible mushrooms.
- Analytical procedures: extraction, separation, quantification (ICP-MS, HPLC speciation).
- Risk assessment against USEPA limits.

## Findings

- **Chromium** — barely bioaccumulates in mushrooms.
- **Antimony** — predominantly in the Sb(V) (less toxic) form.
- **Mercury** — accumulates on contaminated sites; methylmercury is the main hazard.
- **Arsenic** — ~10% of samples exceed inorganic-As limits.
- **Selenium** — "enriched" cultivated mushrooms often overshoot safe thresholds.

## Limitations

- Fragmented data: few standardized methods, almost no work on cooking effects.
- "Safe conditions of consumption" the authors call a still-distant goal — the data are thin.

## Why it matters

The review frames all our "accumulator" cards: not "bad mushroom" but "gathering site + species + element form". For our readers: don't pick on roadsides/industrial land, rotate species, and remember — an "edible" mushroom on polluted ground becomes a different product.
