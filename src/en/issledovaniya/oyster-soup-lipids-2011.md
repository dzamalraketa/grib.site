Schneider I., Kressel G., Meyer A., Krings U., Berger R.G.,
Hahn A. Lipid lowering effects of oyster mushroom (Pleurotus
ostreatus) in humans. Journal of Functional Foods.
2011;3(1):17–24.

## Rationale

Oyster mushrooms contain lovastatin naturally plus β-glucans; whether an ordinary culinary portion shifts lipids needed a controlled feeding test.

## Methods

A randomized intervention: 20 healthy young adults (20–34) ate a daily soup containing 30 g dried oyster mushrooms or a tomato-soup control for 21 days. Fasting lipids and oxidized LDL were measured before and after.

## Results

Versus control, the oyster-soup group showed significant reductions in triglycerides (−0.44 mmol/L, p=0.015) and oxidized LDL (−7.2 U/mL, p=0.013); total cholesterol trended down (−0.47 mmol/L, p=0.059); LDL and HDL unchanged.

## Conclusions

Three weeks of daily oyster-mushroom soup measurably improved two lipid-oxidation-relevant markers — a real food-level effect, modest in size.

## Worth noting

The interesting result is oxidized LDL rather than LDL itself — consistent with an antioxidant rather than absorption mechanism. n=20, young healthy participants, normal-range lipids: effect transfer to hyperlipidemic patients is speculative. The 2026 β-glucan RCT above provides the methodological contrast — bigger, blinder, and null on LDL.
