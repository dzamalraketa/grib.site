Dai X., Stanilka J.M., Rowe C.A., Esteves E.A., Nieves C. Jr, Spaiser S.J., Christman M.C., Langkamp-Henken B., Percival S.S. Consuming Lentinula edodes (Shiitake) Mushrooms Daily Improves Human Immunity: A Randomized Dietary Intervention in Healthy Young Adults. J Am Coll Nutr. 2015;34(6):478–487.

## Rationale

Whole-food dosing is the ecologically real way people consume shiitake; this trial measured what 5–10 g/day of dried mushrooms does to immune-cell function.

## Methods

A randomized 4-week parallel study: 52 healthy volunteers aged 21–41 ate either 5 or 10 g/day of whole dried shiitake. Ex vivo lymphocyte proliferation, cell-surface activation markers, salivary sIgA, CRP and cytokines were measured.

## Results

At both doses: γδ-T-cell proliferation ex vivo rose 60% (p<0.0001), NKT cells doubled (p<0.0001), salivary sIgA secretion increased, C-reactive protein fell. Cytokines shifted toward higher IL-4, IL-10, TNF-α and IL-1α with lower MIP-1α/CCL3; IL-6, IL-1β, MIP-1β, IL-17 and IFN-γ were unchanged.

## Conclusions

Regular culinary shiitake measurably modulates immune-cell function and inflammation markers in healthy people at food-level doses.

## Worth noting

The cytokine pattern — IL-4/IL-10 up, MIP-1α down, CRP down — is consistent with immune modulation rather than raw stimulation. Ex vivo proliferation is a functional measure, stronger than cell counts. A food-level dose (5–10 g dried ≈ a serving or two fresh) makes this directly relevant to eating, not just supplementation.
