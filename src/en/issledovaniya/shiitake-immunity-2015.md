Dai X., Stanilka J.M., Rowe C.A. et al. Consuming Lentinula
edodes (Shiitake) Mushrooms Daily Improves Human Immunity: A
Randomized Dietary Intervention in Healthy Young Adults.
J Am Coll Nutr. 2015;34(6):478–487.

## Rationale

Most immune-mushroom evidence used extracts; this study asked whether ordinary dietary quantities of whole shiitake do the same — the practically relevant question.

## Methods

A randomized parallel dietary intervention: 52 healthy adults (21–41) ate 5 or 10 g/day of dried shiitake for 4 weeks, with ex vivo lymphocyte function, activation-receptor expression, salivary sIgA and CRP as readouts.

## Results

γδ-T-cell ex vivo proliferation rose 60% and NK-T cell proliferation doubled (both p<0.0001); both populations expressed activation receptors more strongly. Salivary secretory IgA increased; C-reactive protein fell.

## Conclusions

A month of eating shiitake at culinary doses produced measurable immune-function gains and lower systemic inflammation in healthy young adults.

## Worth noting

This is the second card covering the same Dai et al. 2015 dataset (the catalog keeps both the immune-marker and dietary-framing writeups). The activation-receptor detail distinguishes this from a simple count increase — the cells were not just more numerous but functionally primed. Food-matrix research like this is what "mushrooms and immunity" claims should cite — not extract data extrapolated to soup.
