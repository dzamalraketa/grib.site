Inaoka T. et al. Improvement of long-term prognosis in patients with ovarian cancers by adjuvant sizofiran immunotherapy: a prospective randomized controlled trial. Biotherapy. 1993;7(1):19-23.

## Why this study

Sizofiran (SPG, schizophyllan) is a β-glucan from *Schizophyllum commune*, registered in Japan as a **medicinal** immunomodulator — not a supplement. Our new "split gill" card is its source species. The question: does adding SPG to chemotherapy improve survival — not just immune markers.

## Methods

- Prospective randomization of **68 ovarian cancer patients**.
- PAC (cisplatin + doxorubicin + cyclophosphamide) ± sizofiran.
- Endpoint — survival, not surrogates.

## Findings

- In stage **Ic–III** patients, survival on **PAC+SPG was significantly higher** than on PAC.
- The benefit appeared in those receiving **≥4 cycles** of PAC+SPG (generalized Kruskal–Wallis p=0.032) and in non-serous adenocarcinomas.
- **No side effects attributable to SPG** were recorded.

## Limitations

- n=68 — a small trial; dated 1993 — chemotherapy standards have moved on.
- Some subgroup analyses sit near the significance threshold (p=0.032–0.076).

## Why it matters

The third "Japanese polysaccharide" in our series after lentinan and PSK: SPG is the rare case where a mushroom β-glucan became a licensed drug (in Japan) and has survival data, not only immune parameters.
