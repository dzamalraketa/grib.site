Environ Microbiol 2018. Isotopic evidence of biotrophy and unusual nitrogen nutrition in soil-dwelling Hygrophoraceae (PMID 30105856).

## Why this study

In our waxcap cards (witch's hat, parrot waxcap) we wrote "saprotroph — or possibly biotroph, the ecology is unclear". This paper is the key evidence for the second version: by their isotopes, waxcaps are **not simply decomposing dead organic matter**.

## Methods

- A field experiment (Sourhope): application of **nitrogen, lime and an organophosphate pesticide** — monitoring fruiting.
- Stable isotope analysis of 15N and 13C in waxcap fruit bodies from **four continents**.
- Plus 14C dating and 13C pulse-labelling.

## Findings

- Nitrogen, lime or pesticide application **suppressed fruiting** — a direct explanation for the disappearance of waxcaps from "improved" grassland.
- The isotope profile of the fruit bodies: **anomalously high 15N and low 13C** — a pattern typical of **mycorrhizal biotrophs**, not saprotrophs.
- The same pattern recurred across four continents — a family-level trait.
- Waxcaps still **cannot be grown in pure culture** — indirect confirmation of dependence on a living partner.

## Why it matters

Waxcaps are not "soil-eaters" but probably **biotrophs with unusual nitrogen metabolism** — possibly tied to meadow plants or mosses. This explains three observations at once: their disappearance under fertiliser, the impossibility of culturing them, and their tie to old undisturbed grassland. The practical takeaway for our cards: a "waxcap meadow" marks soil unfertilised for decades.
