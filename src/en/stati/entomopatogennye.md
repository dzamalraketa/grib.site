## What entomopathogenic fungi are

Entomopathogenic fungi can infect and kill insects. Natural epizootics of these fungi have been known for a long time: in the 19th century entomologists already noted that mummified bees, flies and beetles covered in white or green "fuzz" were not mold but a parasitic fungus in the sporulation phase.

The two species best studied as biopesticides are *Beauveria bassiana* and *Metarhizium anisopliae*. Both infect insects through the cuticle, grow into the body, multiply in the hemolymph and kill the host within days.

## What the studies showed

Stamets devotes a separate chapter of "Mycelium Running" and a series of later publications to entomopathogenic fungi. The main points:

- **Host range.** *Metarhizium anisopliae* attacks termites, bark beetles, locusts, ticks. *Beauveria bassiana* — a broad range of insects, including the Colorado potato beetle and forest pests.
- **Selectivity.** Unlike broad-spectrum chemical insecticides, a specific strain can be tuned to a particular host, reducing collateral damage to beneficial insects.
- **Compatibility with IPM.** Fungal preparations are used in integrated pest management (IPM) schemes alongside biological and agronomic methods.

## Field trials

According to reviews in peer-reviewed journals, preparations based on *Beauveria bassiana* and *Metarhizium anisopliae* proved effective in field conditions for controlling:

- rice weevil (*Sitophilus oryzae*) in grain storage;
- Colorado potato beetle (*Leptinotarsa decemlineata*) in potato fields;
- termites in underground traps;
- *Varroa destructor* mites in hives — here the topic intersects with mycelium extracts for bees (see [Mushrooms and bees]({{ '/en/stati/pchely/' | url }})).

## Limitations and criticism

Stamets emphasizes several limitations often omitted in popular accounts:

- **Dependence on conditions.** Fungal spores are sensitive to ultraviolet light, humidity and temperature. Outdoors, a biopesticide needs repeated applications.
- **Cost and storage.** Live-spore preparations have a limited shelf life and need a cold chain in transport.
- **Regulatory barriers.** Biopesticide registration takes years in different countries; several *Beauveria*- and *Metarhizium*-based products are already registered, and the range keeps growing.

## Our comment

We do not recommend experimenting with entomopathogenic fungi on your own: strains suitable for field use undergo separate selection and registration. Stamets repeatedly stresses that "wild" isolates from the forest are unsuitable as biopesticides — their effectiveness is hard to predict, and they may harbor other microorganisms.
