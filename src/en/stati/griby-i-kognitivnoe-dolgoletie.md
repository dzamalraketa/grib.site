Conversations about mushrooms and the brain often split into two extremes:
aggressive marketing for "brain-boosting capsules" or blanket dismissal. Both
attitudes miss what fungi actually offer. Over the past fifteen years, a
coherent line of human research has emerged — involving not only extracts of
[lion's mane](/en/griby/ezhovik/), but everyday culinary mushrooms from the
grocery store.

## What everyday diet showed: the DaHA cohort (n=663)

In 2019, Barry Halliwell and Lei Feng's team in Singapore published an analysis
of the **Diet and Healthy Aging (DaHA)** cohort — **663 community-dwelling
adults** aged 60 and older ([study card](/en/issledovaniya/mushroom-consumption-mci-2019/),
*Journal of Alzheimer's Disease*, PMID 30775990). They tracked intake of
everyday mushrooms: [golden](/en/griby/veshenka-zolotistaya/),
[oyster](/en/griby/veshenka/), [shiitake](/en/griby/shiitake/) and
[white button](/en/griby/shampinon/) mushrooms.

Participants eating **more than 2 portions of mushrooms per week** (~300 g+
total) had roughly half the odds of mild cognitive impairment (MCI) compared
with those eating less than one portion: **OR = 0.43** (95% CI 0.23–0.78;
p = 0.006). The association held after adjusting for age, education,
hypertension, diabetes, heart disease, physical and social activity.

The honest caveat: this was a cross-sectional study measuring diet and
cognitive status at a single point in time, so it cannot prove causation on its
own.

## Why blood ergothioneine predicts decline (n=470)

The same research group followed up with a prospective cohort of **470 older
memory-clinic patients** in Singapore tracked for up to **5 years** with brain
MRI ([study card](/en/issledovaniya/ergothioneine-cognitive-decline-2022/),
*Antioxidants*, 2022, PMID 36139790).

- Lower baseline plasma **ergothioneine** predicted faster decline across
  memory, attention, executive function and visuomotor speed — specifically in
  non-demented participants at the pre-dementia stage.
- Mediation analysis showed that much of this link was explained by
  cerebrovascular disease burden and brain atrophy. That nuance matters:
  mushroom-derived ergothioneine acts both as a candidate cytoprotectant and as
  a sensitive marker of vascular and metabolic brain health.

## A real-food trial: tamogitake and the gut–brain axis (n=170)

In 2025, a Japanese trial in **170 older adults** (aged 60–79) tested 8 weeks
of dried [golden oyster mushroom (tamogitake)](/en/griby/veshenka-zolotistaya/)
— one of the richest natural sources of ergothioneine — alongside 16S
microbiota sequencing and Cognitrax testing ([study card](/en/issledovaniya/tamogitake-cognition-2025/),
*Frontiers in Nutrition*, PMID 40761356).

Participants whose cognitive scores improved also showed increases in
beneficial gut taxa (*Faecalibacterium*, *Blautia* and others), yet the authors
were candid: there was no universal effect for everyone; response depended on
matching the mushroom substrate to an individual's baseline microbiota.

## A clinical supplement example: lion's mane in MCI (Mori 2009, n=30)

[Lion's mane](/en/griby/ezhovik/) stands apart because of its hericenones and
erinacines, which stimulate nerve growth factor (NGF) synthesis in preclinical
models. In a classic 16-week double-blind RCT by Mori et al.
([study card](/en/issledovaniya/ezhovik-mci-2009/), *Phytotherapy Research*,
2009, PMID 18844328), **30 adults aged 50–80** with diagnosed MCI received
**3 g/day of dry fruiting-body powder** or placebo.

At weeks 8, 12 and 16, cognitive scores (HDS-R) were significantly higher in
the lion's mane group than placebo, but declined again within **4 weeks after
stopping**. Because the sample was small (n = 30) and the trial was run by a
manufacturer's lab, larger independent trials are still needed.

## A balanced takeaway

1. **Start in the kitchen, not the supplement aisle.** Regular culinary
   mushrooms (2+ servings a week in observational data) supply
   [ergothioneine](/en/stati/ergotionein/), which the human body actively
   retains in tissues including the brain.
2. **Do not expect an instant "nootropic surge".** Even in positive lion's mane
   trials, measurable changes took 8–16 weeks and faded after discontinuation.
3. **Brain health is systemic.** Mushrooms complement sleep, exercise, vascular
   care and an overall balanced diet — they do not replace them.

## Limits of knowledge

- The link between 2+ weekly mushroom servings and lower MCI odds comes from
  observational data and awaits long-term randomized dietary trials.
- Pilot RCTs of lion's mane and ergothioneine in MCI remain small and require
  independent multicenter replication.
