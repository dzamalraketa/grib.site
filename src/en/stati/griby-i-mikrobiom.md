Unlike plants, fungi do not build their cell walls out of cellulose. Instead,
their structural scaffold is made of **chitin** (the same polymer found in
crustacean shells and insect exoskeletons) interwoven with branched **beta-1,3/1,6-glucans**.
Human digestive enzymes barely touch this architecture. Yet what escapes
digestion in the small intestine is not wasted: it reaches the colon intact and
serves as substrate for resident gut bacteria.

Biological accuracy matters here: culinary mushrooms do not colonize the gut
like live probiotics. Rather, they act as **prebiotics** — a distinct structural
fiber matrix metabolized by our own microbial community. Three human clinical
studies show what actually happens.

## Study 1: everyday button mushrooms vs meat (Hess & Slavin, 2018)

Researchers at the University of Minnesota ran a randomized crossover trial in
**32 healthy adults** ([study card](/en/issledovaniya/agaricus-bisporus-microbiome-2018/),
*Nutrients*, 2018, PMID 30279332). Each participant completed two 10-day phases:
whole [button mushrooms](/en/griby/shampinon/) twice daily versus a
protein-matched meat diet.

Key measurements:

- **Microbiota shift:** the mushroom phase produced a higher proportion of
  *Bacteroidetes* (p = 0.0002) and lower *Firmicutes* (p = 0.0009) compared with
  meat.
- **Bowel function:** stool weight was significantly higher on mushrooms
  (p = 0.002), consistent with unabsorbed fungal fiber supporting laxation.
- **No shortcuts:** short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs) and stool pH did not change
  over 10 days, and participants reported more mild GI symptoms during the first
  two days — a common response to a sudden fiber increase.

## Study 2: shiitake beta-glucan extract (Morales et al., 2021)

In Madrid, an 8-week double-blind, placebo-controlled trial enrolled **52
adults** with untreated mild hypercholesterolemia (Reducol trial, NCT03550287;
[study card](/en/issledovaniya/shiitake-bge-microbiome-2021/), *Eur J Nutr*,
2021, PMID 33580297). Participants consumed a [shiitake](/en/griby/shiitake/)
extract delivering **3.5 g/day of fungal beta-glucans** or placebo.

The primary hypothesis — lowering blood lipids and inflammatory cytokines
(IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α) — **did not pan out**. However, the intervention safely
raised total fiber intake to cardioprotective levels and **differentially
modulated colonic microbiota** compared with placebo, with specific bacterial
genera correlating with cholesterol metabolism markers.

## Study 3: response depends on baseline microbiota (Hatayama et al., 2025)

A more nuanced picture emerged from a **2025 Japanese intervention** in **170
older adults** aged 60–79 ([study card](/en/issledovaniya/tamogitake-cognition-2025/),
*Frontiers in Nutrition*, PMID 40761356). Over 8 weeks, participants ate
granulated [golden oyster mushroom (tamogitake)](/en/griby/veshenka-zolotistaya/),
moringa leaves or rice bran, alongside 16S microbiota sequencing and Cognitrax
cognitive testing.

The same mushroom did **not** act identically in everyone. Among cognitive
"responders", bacteria linked in the literature to butyrate production and
healthy aging (*Faecalibacterium*, *Blautia*, *Parabacteroides*) shifted
distinctly, whereas non-responders showed different baseline profiles. The
authors' conclusion respects individual biology: effectiveness depends on
matching a food to a person's existing gut ecosystem.

## Practical takeaways

1. **Mushrooms supply a fiber type plants lack.** Most adults fall short on
   dietary fiber; [button](/en/griby/shampinon/), [oyster](/en/griby/veshenka/)
   and [shiitake](/en/griby/shiitake/) mushrooms contribute chitin and branched
   beta-glucans absent from grains and vegetables.
2. **Increase portions gradually.** As Hess 2018 showed, jumping straight to
   large mushroom servings can cause temporary bloating for 1–2 days while the
   microbiota adapts.
3. **Always cook culinary mushrooms.** Heat breaks down heat-labile compounds
   and softens the chitin matrix, improving both safety and digestive comfort.

## Limits of knowledge

- Ten-day trials capture rapid compositional shifts, but long-term stability over
  months remains under-studied.
- Shifts in broad bacterial phyla (*Bacteroidetes/Firmicutes*) are biological
  markers of ecosystem adaptation, not direct proof of disease treatment.
