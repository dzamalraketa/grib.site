When mushrooms are marketed as "functional food", they are often reduced to a
list of isolated compounds. Yet long before reaching our kitchen, a fungus
lives a complex life in forest litter or wood. To survive in an environment full
of bacteria, oxidative stress and competitors, fungi evolved a distinctive
biochemistry: the thiol antioxidant ergothioneine, branched cell-wall
beta-glucans, ergosterol and natural sterols.

Remarkably, humans — who cannot synthesize ergothioneine at all — retained a
dedicated transporter protein, OCTN1, that actively pulls this fungal molecule
from food and holds it in tissues for weeks. On that level, mushrooms on our
plate are not a passing wellness trend but an ancient metabolic partnership.
The scientific question is straightforward: does this biology translate into
measurable human outcomes?

## What the 21-year Malmö cohort showed

The strongest observational signal came not from food questionnaires, but from
direct blood measurements. In the Swedish prospective **Malmö Diet and Cancer**
cohort ([study card](/en/issledovaniya/ergothioneine-cardiovascular-malmo-2020/),
*Heart*, 2020, PMID 31672783), researchers measured 112 plasma metabolites in
**3,236 participants** free of cardiovascular disease and diabetes at baseline
and followed them for a median of **21.4 years**.

Across all 112 metabolites, **ergothioneine** showed the strongest association
with a health-conscious food pattern — and predicted lower risk per 1 SD
increase:

- **coronary artery disease:** **15% lower** risk (HR 0.85);
- **cardiovascular mortality:** **21% lower** (HR 0.79);
- **all-cause mortality:** **14% lower** (HR 0.86).

The honest boundary is clear: an observational cohort shows association, not
proof of causation. Blood ergothioneine may partly reflect a broader pattern of
people who cook fresh meals and include mushrooms regularly.

## Mortality and diet: NHANES and a 5-cohort meta-analysis

In 2021, a Penn State team tested dietary **mushroom intake** directly against
all-cause mortality ([study card](/en/issledovaniya/mushroom-consumption-mortality-nhanes-2021/),
*Nutrition Journal*, 2021, PMID 34548082):

- In the US **NHANES 2003–2014** cohort (n = 30,378; median follow-up 6.7
  years; 2,855 deaths), the point estimate leaned protective but was not
  statistically significant: adjusted HR = **0.84** (95% CI 0.67–1.06).
- Pooling five prospective cohorts (**601,893 participants**), mushroom
  consumption was associated with a modest, statistically significant **6%
  reduction** in all-cause mortality: pooled RR = **0.94** (95% CI 0.91–0.98).

This is a sober, data-respecting finding: culinary mushrooms are not an elixir
of immortality, yet across large populations they consistently accompany a
slightly lower mortality risk.

## Whole mushrooms and cardiometabolic risk: a 22-study review

What happens to blood pressure, glucose and blood lipids when people eat whole
mushrooms such as [button mushrooms](/en/griby/shampinon/),
[oyster mushrooms](/en/griby/veshenka/) or [shiitake](/en/griby/shiitake/)?
Uffelman et al. ([study card](/en/issledovaniya/mushroom-consumption-cardiometabolic-2023/),
*Nutrients*, 2023, PMID 36904079) reviewed **22 human studies** (11
interventional and 11 observational):

- In **experimental trials**, limited evidence pointed to lower
  **triglycerides** and inflammatory marker **hs-CRP**.
- Evidence for blood pressure, fasting glucose and HbA1c remained inconsistent
  or insufficient.
- Most earlier trials scored poorly on NHLBI quality criteria, prompting the
  authors to call for rigorous randomized controlled trials.

## An honest null result: the 2026 oyster mushroom RCT

Exactly such a registered, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial was published
in 2026 by the University of Bonn ([study card](/en/issledovaniya/oyster-betaglucan-ldl-2026/),
*Nutrition & Metabolism*, PMID 42071226). For 4 weeks, **46 adults** with
moderately elevated LDL cholesterol consumed a daily drink containing **8.4 g
of [oyster mushroom](/en/griby/veshenka/) powder** (delivering 3 g of fungal
beta-glucans — matching the EFSA threshold for oat beta-glucans) or placebo.

The primary endpoint — **LDL cholesterol** — did not change. Serum ergosterol
confirmed compliance, and post-hoc analysis suggested lower cholesterol
absorption markers (particularly in women), yet the main takeaway is candid:
branched fungal beta-1,3/1,6-glucans are structurally distinct from linear oat
beta-glucans and did not lower LDL over 4 weeks.

## What this means in practice

1. **Whole mushrooms work best as real food, not a quick-fix pill.** Swapping
   part of red or processed meat for [button mushrooms](/en/griby/shampinon/),
   [oyster mushrooms](/en/griby/veshenka/) or [shiitake](/en/griby/shiitake/)
   naturally lowers energy density while adding potassium, selenium, fiber and
   [ergothioneine](/en/stati/ergotionein/).
2. **Do not expect a rapid drop in cholesterol in one month.** Lipid and blood
   pressure management relies on overall diet, movement and medical care;
   mushrooms are a steady supporting food.
3. **Species variety beats a single "super-mushroom".** Oyster species
   (especially [golden oyster](/en/griby/veshenka-zolotistaya/)) and shiitake
   are rich in ergothioneine, while button mushrooms are affordable year-round.

## Limits of knowledge

- The largest mortality and cardiovascular findings come from **observational
  cohorts**, where residual lifestyle confounding cannot be fully ruled out.
- Short trials (4–8 weeks) may miss slower metabolic shifts; fungal
  beta-glucans require longer, well-powered clinical studies.
