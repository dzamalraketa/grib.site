## Parasitism — not an "exception" but a whole strategy

Our catalogue holds five species whose lives are built on parasitism:
from the blunt "bolete erupting from a potato" to the elegant
Tremella feeding on an invisible host mushroom. Comparing them we
see three distinct tiers.

## Tier 1: parasite of mycorrhiza — Gomphidius and Chroogomphus

**Gomphidius glutinosus** (`mokrukha-elovaya`) and **Chroogomphus
rutilus** (`hroogomfus-ryzhij`) are parasites of the **mycorrhizal
symbiosis**: they plug into the Suillus↔conifer partnership and
siphon off the carbon Suillus receives from the tree.

- **Identification key:** strict linkage to *Suillus granulatus* —
  where that bolete is absent, neither parasite appears. Gomphidius
  is grey-violet with a black print; Chroogomphus is coppery with
  decurrent false-gills.
- **Forager's lesson:** the "shadow" fungus reveals the host: find a
  Gomphidius — look for the weeping bolete beside it.

## Tier 2: parasite of an inconspicuous fungus — Tremella

**Tremella mesenterica** (`tremella-mezentericheskaya`) — the
"yellow brain": a jelly fungus parasitising the **corticioid
fungus *Peniophora*** (an invisible "crust" on twigs). The jelly
grows straight out of the host's substrate — but only it is visible,
not the host.

- **Key:** a yellow jelly mass on dry twigs always means the
  microscopic crust of Peniophora is present (invisible to the
  naked eye).
- **Lesson:** a parasitic fungus can be **prettier** than its host —
  Tremella shows, Peniophora hides.

## Tier 3: parasite of the fruit body — Pseudoboletus and Asterophora

The most spectacular tier: a mushroom growing **straight out of
another mushroom**:

- **Pseudoboletus parasiticus** (`pseudobolet-parazitnyj`) — the
  only parasitic bolete: a yellow bolete erupts from an earthball
  like a tree from a potato. A unique "textbook of mycoparasitism".
- **Asterophora parasitica** (`asterofora-parazitnaya`) — a
  miniature grey cap on **rotting** brittlegills and milk caps:
  the finisher that eats the dying mushroom. Plus chlamydospores —
  a "rainy-day" survival reserve.

## What this means for identification

- **The parasite flags the host**: find a parasitic fungus — look
  for its victim beside it; sometimes the host (a bolete, an
  earthball) is easier to spot.
- **"A mushroom out of a mushroom" is real**: Pseudoboletus and
  Asterophora documentarily prove that one fungus can fruit
  straight out of another's fruit body — "a figure on a figure".
- **Three tiers in one wood**: from the hidden carbon vampire to the
  visible "yellow mash" — the ecological roles are as different as
  the fungi themselves.
