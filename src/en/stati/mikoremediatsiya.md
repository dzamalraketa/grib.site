## What mycoremediation is

Mycoremediation is a branch of bioremediation in which fungi are used to break down pollutants. Most often this involves species that secrete powerful extracellular enzymes — laccases, peroxidases, cytochromes P450. These enzymes can cleave complex organic molecules, including ones bacteria handle poorly.

## What problems it addresses

Areas where mycoremediation has shown results in laboratory and field experiments:

- breakdown of crude-oil components and polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons;
- degradation of organochlorine pesticides and some synthetic dyes;
- sorption of heavy metals (cadmium, lead, arsenic) by fungal biomass;
- filtration of contaminated water through mycelial mats.

## What is important to keep in mind

The effectiveness of a given strain depends on the environment: pH, temperature, oxygen availability, pollutant concentration. There is no "universal" fungus for every situation, and industrial schemes usually rely on a mix of species and careful condition selection. These caveats appear both in academic publications and in materials from commercial companies promoting such solutions.
