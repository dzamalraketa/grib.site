## Latency — the master diagnostic key

Time from meal to symptoms is the fastest way to narrow a mushroom
poisoning. The Diaz 2005 syndrome classification
(`mushroom-poisoning-syndromes-diaz-2005`, 14 syndromes over 28,018
cases) splits all poisonings into two broad groups:

## Group A: early symptoms (minutes — 2 hours) — "good news"

If symptoms arrive quickly, they are usually **not** the lethal
toxins:

- **Muscarinic syndrome** (15–30 min): sweating, tearing, salivation,
  pinpoint pupils, slow pulse. *Clitocybe rivulosa* (our new
  species!), *Inocybe*, *Mycena*. The 2024 mechanism: the toxin is
  "triggered" from a phosphorylated precursor **when the mushroom's
  tissue is destroyed** (`muscarine-release-mechanism-2024`).
  Antidote — atropine.
- **GI syndrome** (30 min–2 h): nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea without
  systemic effects — *Chlorophyllum molybdites* (the No. 1 poisoning
  in the US), *Omphalotus*, bitter bolete, simple indigestion.
- **Coprine/antabuse** (30 min, with alcohol): flushing, tachycardia —
  only in combination with ethanol; *Coprinopsis atramentaria*,
  *Ampulloclitocybe*.
- **Hallucinogenic** (30 min–2 h): visual disturbance, euphoria —
  psilocybin mushrooms; rarely dangerous in itself.

## Group B: late symptoms (6+ hours) — "bad news"

If **more than 6 hours** passed after the meal before vomiting began —
suspect the heavy toxins:

- **Amatoxic (phalloid) syndrome** (6–24 h): a hard GI phase →
  apparent recovery → **liver failure** on days 3–5. *Amanita
  phalloides*, *A. virosa*, *Lepiota brunneoincarnata*, *Pholiotina
  rugosa*, *Galerina*. The 20-year Enjalbert 2002 retrospective
  (`amatoxin-treatment-enjalbert-2002`, 2,108 admissions): the
  protocol's backbone is **silibinin + N-acetylcysteine + forced
  hydration** — the earlier the better; untreated mortality reaches
  10–20%, with transplantation rarely.
- **Orellanine (nephrotoxic) syndrome** (3–14 **days!**): thirst,
  flank pain, oliguria — kidneys fail after a multi-day lag;
  *Cortinarius orellanus*, *C. rubellus*, the newer "accelerated"
  *Amanita smithiana/proxima* (`nephrotoxic-poisoning-diaz-2021`).
  Often leaves chronic kidney disease.
- **Rhabdomyolysis** (1–3 days): muscle pain, dark urine —
  *Tricholoma equestre* and *Russula subnigricans*; first described
  as a syndrome by Diaz.

## First aid: what actually works

Comparing all five works yields one protocol:

1. **Call an ambulance at once** — do not "wait till morning": in
   late syndromes hours decide the outcome.
2. **Flush the stomach** and give sorbent — only if the patient is
   conscious.
3. **Keep mushroom remains** (raw, unwashed) — the species
   speeds protocol choice.
4. **Do not "treat" with milk/oil** — none of the reviews found
   benefit in folk remedies.
5. Note the **time of the meal** — that is the diagnostic key:
   "ate 3 hours ago" and "ate yesterday" are different worlds.

## Outcome statistics

The meta-analysis `mushroom-poisoning-mortality-meta-2023` (33
studies): pooled mortality of all mushroom poisonings — **2.87%**;
of 16 liver transplants for amatoxin, **14 survived**. The lesson:
even the "textbook-lethal" phalloid syndrome is survivable in ~88%
with timely care — but "timely" means the first hours, not days.
