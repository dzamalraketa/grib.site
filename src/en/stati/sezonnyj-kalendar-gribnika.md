The dates in this article are **guideposts for the temperate zone**: the real start depends on the year, temperature and rainfall — a 2–3 week shift is normal. The periods come from the "season" fields of our catalog cards. No table replaces the skill of identifying a mushroom: **the rule "not sure — don't take it" always applies.**

## Spring: April–May

| Mushroom | Status | What matters |
|---|---|---|
| [Morel](/en/griby/smorchok/) | conditionally edible | a 2–3-week window right after snowmelt; only after boiling |

Spring is the "quiet" season: fewer toxic lookalikes, but the morel in particular must never be eaten raw or undercooked (see the card).

## Summer: July–August

Peak season. Also appearing are [milkcap](/en/griby/gruzd/) (conditionally edible, needs soaking and salting), [belyanka](/en/griby/belyanka/), [saffron milkcap](/en/griby/ryzhik/), [beefsteak fungus](/en/griby/pechenochnitsa/), [Caesar's mushroom](/en/griby/caesarev-grib/) (further south), [black trumpet chanterelle](/en/griby/chernaya-lisichka/).

**Warning:** this is exactly when the [death cap](/en/griby/blednaya-poganka/) appears — confused with button mushrooms, russulas and parasols. To see the telltale sign — a stem inside a sac-like volva — the mushroom must be **dug up, not cut**.

## Autumn: September–October

| Mushroom | Status | Dangerous lookalike |
|---|---|---|
| [Autumn honey fungus](/en/griby/openyosenniy/) | conditionally edible | [funeral bell (Galerina marginata)](/en/griby/galerina-okaymlennaya/) |
| [Volnushka](/en/griby/volnushka/) | conditionally edible | — |
| [Milkcap](/en/griby/gruzd/) | conditionally edible | other milkcaps (also conditionally edible) |
| [Maitake](/en/griby/maitake/) | edible | — |
| [Oyster mushroom](/en/griby/veshenka/) | edible | — |

Autumn is the main season of **honey-fungus mistakes**: Galerina contains the same amatoxins as the death cap and grows on the same stumps at the same time. A reliable test is the spore print: white in honey fungus, rusty-brown in Galerina.

Separately on honey fungi: in a 25-year series of poison-center calls in Wisconsin, USA (18 cases, half mycologist-confirmed), 83% had gastrointestinal symptoms with no organ complications or deaths — but poisonings happened **even after cooking**. The practical takeaway: honey fungi are always **boiled long, with the water discarded**, and on first acquaintance not eaten in quantity.

## Late autumn and winter: November–March

[Oyster mushroom](/en/griby/veshenka/) keeps growing until December and, in mild winters, until spring. During thaws you can also find [winter honey fungus (enoki)](/en/griby/enoki/) — the wild form differs from the white cultivated "needle" one.

Cultivated forms are available year-round: [button mushroom](/en/griby/shampinon/), [shiitake](/en/griby/shiitake/), [tremella](/en/griby/tremella/). Medicinal species such as [reishi](/en/griby/reishi/) and [chaga](/en/griby/chaga/) have no strict picking season, but their use is a separate safety topic (see the [drug-interaction map](/en/stati/gribnye-dobavki-vzaimodejstviya/)).

## Three rules worth memorizing

1. **After 6 hours — seek medical help.** Vomiting and diarrhea starting 6–24 hours after wild mushrooms is the typical picture of amatoxin poisoning; a "false recovery" then follows (~36–48 hours) while the liver is being destroyed (PMC11640968). Call emergency services and keep leftover mushrooms.
2. **Cooking does not save you from amatoxins.** They are heat-stable, so a death cap or Galerina cannot be "boiled safe."
3. **Do not pick what you cannot positively identify.** For gilled mushrooms with a ring and white gills — dig out the entire stem.
