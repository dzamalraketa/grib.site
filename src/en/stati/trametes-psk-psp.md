## What this is about

*Trametes versicolor* (Turkey Tail, Yun Zhi) is a wood-decay fungus with a concentrically banded cap that has been studied for decades in Japan and China as the source of two polysaccharide fractions:

- **PSK** (polysaccharide-K, "Krestin") — a protein-bound β-(1→3)/(1→6)-glucan isolated from *T. versicolor* mycelium strain CM-101 in Japan. Registered as an adjuvant drug in Japan since the 1970s.
- **PSP** (polysaccharide-peptide) — a similar fraction developed in China on the Cov-1 strain.

Both fractions belong to **adjuvant therapy**: they are prescribed not instead of but together with standard treatment (surgery, chemotherapy, radiotherapy). The species card is at [Trametes versicolor]({{ '/en/griby/trametes-versicolor/' | url }}).

## How it works pharmacologically

PSK and PSP are not "cancer drugs" in the direct sense. Their action is attributed to several mechanisms:

- **Immunomodulation.** Stimulation of macrophages, NK cells, T-lymphocytes; increased production of interleukins and interferons.
- **Apoptosis modulation.** In laboratory work — increased tumour-cell sensitivity to chemotherapeutic agents.
- **Antioxidant protection.** Reduced oxidative stress, including that caused by chemotherapy.
- **Microbiota effects.** Altered gut-microbiota composition, indirectly influencing the immune response.

These data are collected in the review on <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">PubMed on PSK and adjuvant therapy</a> and in our article [Beta-glucans and immunity]({{ '/en/stati/betaglyukany/' | url }}), which also describes the general biochemistry of β-glucans.

## Clinical data: what studies showed

### Gastric cancer

The most extensive evidence base is for **gastric cancer**. A series of Japanese RCTs (mostly from the 1980s–2000s) showed that adding PSK to standard chemotherapy improves 5-year survival of patients after radical surgery. In the <a href="https://www.cochranelibrary.com" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Cochrane review on PSK for gastric cancer</a>, these data are acknowledged as statistically significant, with a caveat about the quality of individual studies (no blinding, small samples).

### Colorectal cancer

For **colorectal cancer**, results of several Phase III RCTs were published in which PSK was added to adjuvant chemotherapy. Some studies showed improved relapse-free survival; results differ across patient subgroups. Meta-analyses confirm the general trend but point to the need for larger modern trials.

### Other nosologies

PSK and PSP have also been studied in lung, breast, liver and prostate cancer. Individual studies recorded improved survival and quality-of-life measures, but study designs vary, and no large multicentre RCTs meeting modern standards have been published.

### Quality of life and side effects

Several studies address the effect of PSK/PSP on the quality of life of patients undergoing chemotherapy. Some noted reduced fatigue, improved appetite and subjective tolerability of treatment. Side effects in the available studies are rare and limited to mild dyspeptic symptoms.

## Context for these data

- **Age of the studies.** Most PSK RCTs were conducted
  in the 1980s–2000s. In the decades since, new
  work and refined protocols have appeared; the overall
  picture is gradually expanding.
- **Product heterogeneity.** PSK and PSP are not
  the same substance. Differences in strains, extraction methods and
  standardization reflect the products' long evolution.
- **Geography of registration.** In Japan, China and several Asian
  countries PSK is registered as an adjuvant agent. In Europe, the US
  and Russia it is registered in the dietary-supplement
  category.
- **Population data.** Most studies were conducted
  in Asian populations; in 2020–2025 work
  in other regions has appeared.

These limitations are reproduced in the review [Trametes versicolor and the PSK/PSP work]({{ '/en/temy/immunitet/' | url }}) in the [Mushrooms and immunity]({{ '/en/temy/immunitet/' | url }}) topic hub, and in the [Beta-glucans and immunity]({{ '/en/stati/betaglyukany/' | url }}) review.

## What is important to keep in mind

- **PSK and PSP are adjuvants, not a replacement for standard therapy.**
  In Japan and China PSK is part of approved adjuvant-therapy
  protocols for oncology; in European countries, North
  America and Russia PSK and PSP are registered
  as dietary supplements.
- **Standardization is critical.** Different commercial products
  labelled "Turkey Tail" or "Yun Zhi" contain different
  strains and different fractions, making them
  incomparable.
- **The evidence base is expanding.** In 2020–2025
  new RCTs have appeared, including in Europe and North
  America — the overall picture is gradually being refined.
