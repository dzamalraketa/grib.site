## Three studies — three levels of the question

The question "is the mushroom clean" splits into three independent
answers — and three papers supply them in sequence.

### Study 1: mercury in the gypsy mushroom — Falandysz 2014

A field study of **20 Polish populations** of *Cortinarius caperatus*
(`caperatus-mercury-falandysz-2014`):

- Median cap Hg: **0.81–2.4 mg/kg** dry mass; stipes ~2.5× lower.
- Bioconcentration factor: **18–120** — the fungus accumulates mercury
  **above the background soil** even in clean forests.
- The authors' conclusion: episodic consumption is safe;
  **frequent seasonal consumption is not**.

### Study 2: element speciation — the 2025 review

The systematic review *Decoding trace element speciation in mushrooms*
(`mushroom-trace-elements-2025`, Food Chem, PMID 39357309):

- Elements exist in mushrooms in different **chemical forms**
  (speciation): inorganic As is more toxic than organic forms;
  methylmercury is worse than Hg²⁺.
- **~10% of samples** in the analysed studies exceeded arsenic limits;
  selenium is a useful micronutrient but within a narrow corridor.
- Cadmium, lead and mercury are the main "dirty three" for wild
  mushroom foragers.

### Study 3: the urban Russula — Chemosphere 2020

*R. cyanoxantha* across the Cluj urbanisation gradient
(`russula-urban-metals-2020`):

- **Counter-intuitively**, urban samples held **less** cadmium
  (0.39 mg/kg) than forest ones (3.15–14.1 mg/kg).
- Conclusion: accumulation is set by **the specific soil**
  (pH, geology, mushroom species), not a "city/forest" binary.

## What follows — practical rules

Comparing the three works yields a consistent picture:

1. **There is no "geographically clean mushroom"** — only a "clean
   substrate": cadmium was higher in the *forest* Russula than the
   *urban* one.
2. **An accumulator species matters more than the site**: the gypsy
   mushroom picks up mercury **even in clean forests** — it is a
   species trait, not pollution.
3. **Speciation decides**: 1 mg/kg of arsenic in different forms is
   both harmless and toxic; "total As" without speciation is
   an incomplete picture.
4. **Practice:** an edible species is no licence for a "seasonal
   stockpile": rotate species, do not make one accumulator mushroom
   the base of your diet, and do not pick from knowingly polluted
   industrial ground.

## Limits of knowledge

- Most works are **observational** (one sample — one point),
  so causality is limited.
- Speciation analyses are expensive — we have few data on element
  *forms* in wild populations.
- Standard EU limits for Cd/Hg in mushrooms cover **cultivated**
  species; wild accumulators barely feature in them.
