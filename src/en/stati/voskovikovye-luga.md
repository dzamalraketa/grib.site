## A biome that is not on the map

A "waxcap grassland" is the ecological term for old grass fields
that have gone **decades without fertiliser**. On such soil a whole
community of waxcaps assembles — and it is this community that makes
the meadow "waxcap". In Britain a few hectares survive; in Russia
they persist in the Caucasus, the Carpathians and old meadows of
the Centre.

## Why "unfertilised" is a condition of life

The 2018 isotope study (`waxcap-biotrophy-isotope-2018`) showed
waxcaps are **not saprotrophs** but biotrophs with unusual nitrogen
nutrition (probably tied to meadow mosses or plants). The direct
consequence is their nitrogen sensitivity:

- Applications of **nitrogen fertiliser, lime or pesticide**
  suppress fruiting entirely.
- A meadow "improved" — the waxcaps are gone for good: recovery
  takes decades of withholding fertiliser.
- A waxcap grassland is therefore **living memory** that this soil
  has not been touched by nitrogen for 50–100+ years.

## Three catalogue waxcaps — three "colour codes"

| Species | Colour | Signal |
|---|---|---|
| **Hygrocybe conica** (witch's hat) | red → black on touch | old-meadow indicator |
| **Gliophorus psittacinus** (parrot) | emerald → ochre (chameleon) | untouched mossy field |
| **Hygrocybe coccinea** (scarlet) | saturated red | a "gold-standard" waxcap meadow |

Each species is its own "maturity marker" of the meadow: conica and
psittacinus are baseline indicators; coccinea marks a meadow of the
highest waxcap value.

## A meadow as an open-air museum

A waxcap grassland is rare not "because the mushrooms are few" but
because the **condition** of their life is incompatible with modern
farming. It is a "chronometer without nitrogen": from the waxcap
species list a botanist can read a field's history — when it was
last fertilised, ploughed or mown the new way.

## What to do on encounter

- **Do not pick**: waxcaps are not an edible target but a rare
  ecosystem; a photograph kills less than a basket.
- **Record the site**: waxcap grasslands are entered into regional
  registers of protected grass communities.
- **Do not "improve" it**: any nitrogen dressing destroys the
  waxcap community for good.
